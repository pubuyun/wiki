### Opening

Welcome to the guided exploration of the male reproductive system tactile graphic. Before we begin, place the graphic in front of you with the raised structures facing upward. Next, feel the surface of the graphic to locate the arrow. Rotate the graphic until the long, narrow end of the arrow points toward your body. Once positioned, keep the graphic in place. The arrow should now be located in the lower-left corner. Throughout this guide, “straight up” refers to the direction away from your body, while “straight down” refers to the direction toward your body. “Left” and “right” correspond to your left-hand side and right-hand side, respectively. Next, we will explore the penis, erectile tissue, scrotum, testes, epididymides, vas deferens, urinary bladder, prostate gland, and urethra in order, learning about their positions and main functions.

### 1. Penis

Starting from the arrow, slowly move both hands straight upward. As you move, you will first encounter a horizontal dividing line. Continue moving upward past this line to enter the male reproductive system region of the graphic. Now, move your fingers to the lower central region of the graphic. Here, you will encounter a relatively long, thick, vertically raised structure with an elongated shape. Slowly trace this structure with your fingers, feeling its length and outer outline. This structure represents the penis. The penis is one of the male external reproductive organs. It mainly consists of erectile tissue, connective tissue, blood vessels, nerves, and the urethra. It is involved in both reproduction and urination. The urethra inside the penis allows urine to leave the body and also serves as the passage through which semen is expelled during ejaculation. The graphic uses a long, vertically raised structure to help us understand the overall shape of the penis and its position within the male reproductive system.

### 2. Erectile Tissue

Now, keep your fingers within the region of the penis. Slowly move your fingers along its surface. You will encounter several longitudinal raised structures that differ from the outer outline of the penis. Slowly trace these raised areas, feeling how they extend along the length of the penis. These structures represent the erectile tissue inside the penis. The erectile tissue mainly consists of two corpora cavernosa and one corpus spongiosum, with the corpus spongiosum surrounding the urethra within the penis. Erectile tissue contains numerous blood vessels and sponge-like spaces that can fill with blood. When appropriately stimulated, blood flows into these spaces, causing the erectile tissue to expand and become engorged. This increases the size and firmness of the penis. Erectile tissue plays an important role in penile erection, while the corpus spongiosum also helps keep the urethra open during an erection. Please note that the graphic uses prominent longitudinal raised structures to simplify the representation of erectile tissue and does not reproduce its actual microscopic structure. After exploring these structures, move your fingers back to the base of the penis.

### 3. Scrotum

Now, starting from the base of the penis, slowly move your fingers toward the left and right sides. In the lower region of the graphic, you will encounter two rounded, raised areas positioned close to each other. Slowly trace the outer outlines of these raised areas, feeling their shapes and positions. Together, these structures represent the scrotum. The scrotum is a pouch of skin located below the base of the penis. It is internally divided into left and right compartments by a septum, with each compartment typically containing one testis. The main functions of the scrotum are to contain and protect the testes and help regulate the temperature around them. Normal sperm production generally requires a temperature slightly lower than the body’s core temperature. Through the contraction and relaxation of its skin and the activity of associated muscles, the scrotum can adjust the distance between the testes and the body, helping maintain a suitable temperature. The graphic uses two rounded, raised areas to help us understand the position of the scrotum and its roles in protection and temperature regulation.

### 4. Testes

Now, move your fingers to one side of the scrotal region. Within this area, you will encounter a relatively large, oval-shaped raised structure with a fairly smooth surface. Slowly trace its outer outline with your fingers, feeling its rounded shape. This structure represents a testis. Next, move your fingers to the other side of the scrotal region and locate a similar oval-shaped structure. Males typically have two testes, located on the left and right sides of the scrotum. The testes are the primary male reproductive organs and perform two important functions: producing sperm and secreting androgens. Sperm are mainly produced within the seminiferous tubules inside the testes, while androgens such as testosterone are primarily secreted by interstitial cells, also known as Leydig cells. Testosterone contributes to the development of male reproductive organs, the formation of secondary sexual characteristics, and the regulation of sperm production. Please note that the two testes may differ slightly in size and position and are not necessarily perfectly symmetrical. The graphic uses two oval-shaped raised structures to help us understand the positions and main functions of the testes.

### 5. Epididymides

Now, choose one testis and slowly move your fingers along its outer outline. From the edge of the testis, explore upward and outward. You will encounter a thinner, curved, elongated structure located close to the edge of the testis. Slowly trace this curved structure, noticing how its shape differs from that of the testis. This structure represents the epididymis. The epididymis is a highly coiled tube located mainly along the posterior side of the testis. It is generally divided into three regions: the head, body, and tail. After sperm are produced in the testes, they enter the epididymis, where they undergo further maturation and gradually acquire the ability to move normally and fertilize an egg. The epididymis also serves as an important site for the temporary storage of sperm. During ejaculation, sperm travel from the epididymis into the vas deferens, continuing their journey through the male reproductive tract. The graphic uses a thin, curved structure positioned close to the edge of the testis to help us distinguish the epididymis from the testis and understand its role in sperm maturation and storage.

### 6. Vas Deferens

Now, keep your fingers near the epididymis and locate a long, narrow tube extending away from it. Slowly trace this structure straight upward, feeling how it extends from the region near the testis. Continue moving your fingers along the tube, noticing its length and direction. This structure represents the vas deferens. The vas deferens is a muscular tube with relatively thick walls, mainly responsible for transporting sperm from the epididymis toward the subsequent parts of the ejaculatory pathway. It begins at the tail of the epididymis, extends upward within the spermatic cord, passes through the inguinal canal into the pelvic cavity, and joins the duct of the seminal vesicle behind the urinary bladder to form the ejaculatory duct. During ejaculation, rhythmic contractions of the smooth muscle in the walls of the vas deferens propel sperm forward. The sperm then mix with secretions from glands such as the seminal vesicles and prostate gland to form semen. Please note that the vas deferens does not connect directly to the urinary bladder and does not transport urine. The graphic uses a long, narrow tube extending upward from the epididymis to help us understand the position of the vas deferens and its role in sperm transport.

### 7. Urinary Bladder

Now, continue exploring upward along the vas deferens. As your fingers reach the upper central region of the graphic, you will encounter a relatively large, dome-shaped raised structure. Slowly trace its outer outline, feeling its broad, rounded shape. This structure represents the urinary bladder. The urinary bladder is an elastic, hollow muscular organ located within the pelvic cavity. Its main function is to temporarily store urine produced by the kidneys and transported through the ureters. As the bladder gradually fills with urine, receptors in its walls detect the stretching of the bladder and contribute to the initiation of the urination reflex. Under appropriate conditions, the detrusor muscle in the bladder wall contracts while the urethral sphincters relax, allowing urine to leave the body through the urethra. Please note that the urinary bladder belongs to the urinary system and is not a male reproductive organ. However, because it is anatomically close to several structures of the male reproductive system, including the prostate gland and vas deferens, it is also included in this graphic to help us understand the spatial relationship between the urinary and reproductive systems.

### 8. Prostate Gland

Now, slowly move your fingers straight downward from the urinary bladder toward the central region of the graphic. Directly beneath the bladder, you will encounter a raised structure that is noticeably smaller than the bladder, with a relatively rounded outline. Slowly trace its edge, feeling its position and shape. This structure represents the prostate gland. The prostate is a gland within the male reproductive system, located below the urinary bladder. It surrounds the section of the urethra closest to the bladder outlet, known as the prostatic urethra. The main function of the prostate gland is to secrete prostatic fluid, which forms part of semen. Prostatic fluid contains various substances, including enzymes and mineral ions, that help maintain the normal properties of semen and support sperm activity. During ejaculation, smooth muscles surrounding the prostate contract, helping release prostatic fluid into the urethra, where it mixes with other reproductive tract secretions. Because the prostate surrounds part of the urethra, abnormal enlargement of the prostate may also interfere with normal urination. The graphic uses a smaller raised structure beneath the bladder to help us understand the position of the prostate gland and its role in semen formation.

### 9. Urethra

Finally, move your fingers to the central region of the prostate gland and locate a long, narrow tube-like structure. Starting from this position, slowly trace the structure straight downward. You will notice that this tube extends from the lower part of the urinary bladder, passes through the prostate gland, and continues downward into the penis. This structure represents the urethra. The urethra is a tube connecting the urinary bladder to the outside of the body, primarily responsible for carrying urine out of the body. In the male reproductive system, it also serves as the passage through which semen is expelled. The male urethra is generally divided into three regions: the prostatic urethra, membranous urethra, and spongy urethra. The spongy urethra extends through the penis and eventually opens at its tip. During ejaculation, sperm mix with fluids secreted by glands of the reproductive system to form semen, which is then expelled through the urethra. Although urine and semen share part of the same passage, during normal ejaculation, the internal urethral sphincter at the bladder neck contracts. This helps prevent semen from entering the bladder and reduces the likelihood of urine and semen being expelled simultaneously. The graphic uses a tube-like structure extending downward from the bladder, passing through the prostate gland, and entering the penis to help us understand the position of the urethra and its dual functions in the urinary and reproductive systems.
