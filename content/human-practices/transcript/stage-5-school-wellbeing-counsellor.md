**Ms. Maria**

I am not an expert on the scientific material in the first part, so I will focus mainly on the later section.

Before that, who is the handbook intended for? You mentioned the general public, but the design seems more suited to students. I like it because I work with teenagers. However, if you want adults to see it as a professional resource, you may want to reconsider the colors, flowers, and hearts.

Could you also explain the front cover? Who designed it, and why was it designed this way?

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

It features our team mascot, which represents our project. We wanted the design to feel inclusive and welcoming, giving readers a sense of warmth and belonging in the community.

**Ms. Maria**

That helps me understand the design.

Regarding the section on talking to close friends about hygiene, the approach should be kind, respectful, and direct. We call this kind directness.

Rather than beginning with “I'm not sure if you've noticed,” you could first say that you would like to share something because you care about them and would rather they hear it from you than from someone else. Ask to speak privately, then have the conversation.

I am less comfortable with the indirect approach suggested in the handbook because it could cause confusion. My preferred approach would be direct, kind, and respectful.

Another suggestion is to replace “Would you like me to come with you to talk to a teacher?” with “Would you like me to come with you to talk to a trusted adult?” The student may prefer a counselor or another adult rather than a teacher.

It would also be helpful to ask someone else to review the handbook's grammar and the flow of its ideas.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

In your experience, do students come to you because they have body odor or have been bullied about it? How do they feel, and what advice would you give them?

**Ms. Maria**

In my experience, it is very rare for a student to come here and say directly that they have body odor. More often, someone else notices it.

When discussing wellbeing, I may ask about their daily routines and hygiene, then use that conversation to explore how they feel about their body. I try to approach these conversations with kind directness and respect.

Even if I have not noticed the odor myself, I can learn from the student about how they care for their body, what they eat, and what they do. Teachers can also help by observing and supporting them.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Is it common for teachers to refer students to you because of noticeable odor in class?

**Ms. Maria**

Teachers do make observations. If they notice a recurring issue over time, rather than something on just one occasion, they may discuss it with the counselor, who can then have a conversation with the student.

At my former school, we also had hygiene talks for teenagers. Those provided an opportunity to discuss these topics.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

There can be a misunderstanding that all body odor is caused by poor hygiene. Our project focuses on a genetic contribution to underarm odor. Some students maintain good hygiene but still have noticeable odor and may face teasing or discrimination. How should that situation be approached?

**Ms. Maria**

That is an important distinction, but how would I know that genetics is the cause unless I had that information?

This is why the conversation should also involve a doctor. As a counselor, I would not be able to establish the biological cause myself. If I had that background information, it would inform the conversation. Based on my own experience, the conversations I have had have mainly focused on hygiene.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Do you think the social environment is inclusive enough for conversations about body odor, or is it generally treated negatively?

**Ms. Maria**

It can be difficult, but it is a conversation that needs to happen, just as a conversation about bad breath might need to happen.

Avoiding a difficult conversation does not help the person. A respectful conversation can support them and help them feel part of the group rather than excluded.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

If a student is being excluded because of body odor, could someone standing up for them and including them encourage other students to accept them too?

**Ms. Maria**

It is similar to responding to bullying. Someone who stands up rather than remaining a bystander can help stop it. Supporting that person can also encourage others to change their perspective.

Respect matters throughout. You may notice someone else's odor, but you should still treat them respectfully. If you know something that could help, you can share it sensitively.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

We are considering printing the handbook and placing it in a corner of the school so students can pick it up and find useful information. Would that help?

**Ms. Maria**

Having it available for students to read themselves is more respectful than reading it aloud or drawing attention to a particular person. It allows them to explore the information privately.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What emotional effects might arise when other people talk about someone's body odor behind their back?

**Ms. Maria**

It can affect mental wellbeing and self-esteem. The person may already be trying to address the issue, and if it has a genetic basis, those comments can be even more hurtful.

It is important to consider how vulnerable someone may feel in that situation.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What other educational formats could help students understand body odor?

**Ms. Maria**

At my former school, we focused mainly on hygiene education. Talks for teenagers could cover bodily changes, menstruation, and related topics.

Alongside the handbook, you could make a short video explaining the topic, the changes that occur during puberty, why odor can develop, and what students can do. The approach should help them understand their bodies rather than reinforce stereotypes about smelling unpleasant.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Do students come to you directly about anxiety or a loss of confidence caused by body odor? How would you support them?

**Ms. Maria**

That has not been a common direct concern in my experience. Support can begin with self-awareness, followed by identifying practical actions.

For example, a conversation might include routines such as washing clothes, because those can also contribute to odor. It is also important to help the person build self-esteem and confidence.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Apart from what the student can do, what can bystanders do to help?

**Ms. Maria**

Be observant. Do not join in when people say hurtful things about someone else. If you feel able to speak up, tell them to stop. Standing up for someone takes courage, but it can help.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What could the school and wider community do to support these students and reduce discrimination?

**Ms. Maria**

Including the topic in PSHE would already be a form of support because it communicates that the issue matters. The discussion can go beyond hygiene to address the social concerns around it.

Students can avoid being passive bystanders. Teachers can be observant, help students, have difficult conversations sensitively, and refer them to us when appropriate.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Thank you for your feedback and advice.
