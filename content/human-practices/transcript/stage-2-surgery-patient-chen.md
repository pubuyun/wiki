**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

When did you first notice that you had underarm odor?

**Ms. Chen**

Around puberty, probably in my first or second year of middle school. It was more noticeable when I first discovered it. After puberty, the odor became less noticeable when I was not sweating.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Before surgery, would you describe your underarm odor as mild, moderate, or severe?

**Ms. Chen**

Moderate.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How did it affect your daily life, such as social interactions, work, studies, or emotional wellbeing?

**Ms. Chen**

It was not much of a problem at school, but I became more concerned when I started working. It could be inconvenient. For example, I would avoid raising my arms in summer.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What methods did you use before surgery?

**Ms. Chen**

When I was a student, I used a lightly scented roll-on antiperspirant.

Now I also use a spray recommended by a friend who has underarm odor but has not had surgery. She regularly has laser hair removal and uses this spray, which works well for her. I occasionally still have some odor after surgery, and the spray keeps it under control for about a week.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

That sounds effective.

**Ms. Chen**

Yes, but you need to shave your underarm hair thoroughly. Otherwise, the spray sticks to the hair rather than the skin, which reduces its effectiveness.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Apart from roll-ons and sprays, have you tried anything else?

**Ms. Chen**

I have used sulfur soap to wash my underarms while bathing.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How effective were these methods?

**Ms. Chen**

I think the spray worked better. Sulfur soap did not help much; it just left a sulfur smell after washing. Roll-ons can work for people who do not sweat much, but they are less suitable for heavy sweating. With scented roll-ons in particular, the fragrance can mix with the smell of sweat, which is unpleasant.

I now use an unscented Japanese deodorant cream that works very well. One application lasts about 24 hours. I still sweat, but there is no noticeable odor even when sweating. That may partly be because I have had surgery.

I occasionally still notice some odor. Before surgery, the doctor explained that the underarm sweat glands are extensive, so some odor might remain afterward. I had a minimally invasive procedure rather than a more extensive excision. Surgery can greatly improve the problem, but I do not think a complete cure is realistic.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What drawbacks have you experienced with these products?

**Ms. Chen**

A spray prescribed by a doctor caused itching and redness, almost like an allergic reaction. I have not experienced any side effects from the spray my friend recommended.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What made you decide to have surgery?

**Ms. Chen**

I was thinking about getting married and having children. The doctor told me it would be best to avoid those products during pregnancy because of the precautions and concerns involved. It felt similar to being advised to have problematic wisdom teeth removed before pregnancy.

I worried that without surgery, I would have odor during pregnancy if I could not use those products. I also expected that being unable to bathe regularly during postpartum confinement would affect my daily life. Since the procedure was minimally invasive, I decided to try it.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How much did the surgery cost?

**Ms. Chen**

A little over RMB 10,000, as far as I remember. I did not need to stay in hospital and went home afterward. It was performed by the plastic surgery department at Peking University Hospital.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Looking back, do you think the surgery was worth the cost?

**Ms. Chen**

Yes. Immediately afterward, the odor was completely gone, so the improvement was obvious. I no longer had to keep thinking about applying deodorant. I also sweated less. I still sweated, but there was no odor.

Now there is a little odor on one side and none on the other. I wonder whether that difference might be related to the surgeon's technique.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Did the procedure go smoothly?

**Ms. Chen**

Yes. I was awake throughout because it was done under local anesthesia, and the operation took nearly two hours.

The doctor told me not to shave beforehand because areas with denser hair indicated where I sweated more. I was given an anesthetic cream to apply to the treatment area about 15 to 30 minutes before surgery.

On the operating table, after injecting the local anesthetic, the doctor made a small opening in my underarm and inserted an instrument. During the procedure, the doctor asked whether I needed more anesthetic. I did not feel much pain during the operation, although the anesthetic injections hurt.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How long did recovery take? Was that within the expected timeframe?

**Ms. Chen**

About a month, which was the normal recovery period.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Did you experience any adverse reactions or discomfort during recovery?

**Ms. Chen**

Recovery went smoothly, but my movement was restricted. During the first week, I had to wear a garment that held my upper body in a fixed position. Bathing was inconvenient, so I could only wipe myself down or rinse briefly.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Did you need ongoing treatment after the operation?

**Ms. Chen**

No.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How long after surgery did you notice the odor returning?

**Ms. Chen**

Several years later. There is practically no odor in winter because it depends on sweating. In very hot summer weather, there can be a little odor.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

If our proposed synthetic biology approach could provide non-invasive odor control through regular application, with fewer side effects, would you be willing to try it?

**Ms. Chen**

Definitely. Quite a few people in China have underarm odor, especially in summer. Many people have at least some odor when they sweat heavily. If your product works and has few side effects, as you describe, I think it could appeal to people.

However, there are already many deodorant products on the market. I only discovered my current cream through a recommendation that appeared on my phone. Whenever I visit Chung Ying Street, I stock up on a few because each one lasts a long time.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Market competition is something we need to consider carefully. What product format would you prefer?

**Ms. Chen**

A cream could be a good direction to explore because it lasts longer than a spray. It is easy to use too much spray and finish it quickly.

With a cream, it depends on how often you apply it, but I use mine once a day, and one tube lasts around six months or even longer. It is lightweight and convenient for business trips. I also find it easier to take through airport security, whereas sprays may prompt questions about their volume.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Which aspects of a new product matter most to you?

**Ms. Chen**

Effectiveness and how long it lasts. I would like it to keep odor under control throughout a full day, even if I keep sweating.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Is there anything we have overlooked, or any other advice you would like to give us?

**Ms. Chen**

Are there any established products on the market that use the same approach, or is this something you are developing independently?

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

As far as we know, there are currently no products on the market using synthetic biology in this way to address underarm odor. Most existing approaches involve antiperspirants, botulinum toxin injections, or surgery.

**Ms. Chen**

A maternal and child health hospital in Yantian recently introduced botulinum toxin injections for this. However, I understand that injections do not provide a permanent cure. Surgery is not necessarily a complete cure either, because some sweat glands may remain, and I believe they can grow back.

Interestingly, your uncle's underarm odor has become less noticeable as he has grown older.

Could you explain the difference between more extensive excision surgery and the minimally invasive procedure you had?

**Ms. Chen**

The more extensive procedure removes a large area of underarm sweat glands. It leaves a larger wound and a long scar, takes longer to recover from, and may still not eliminate the problem completely.

I had a minimally invasive procedure. The wound has healed, and I can barely see it now. The doctor made a small opening near the underarm and inserted an instrument to scrape out the sweat glands.

Afterward, I had to wear a very tight garment around my shoulders and underarms for a week, with gauze packed under my arms and secured firmly. I was told to avoid sweating, so I spent that week in an air-conditioned room.

After a week, I returned to the hospital for the doctor to check my recovery, and I could stop wearing the garment. I was not allowed to raise my arms during the first month. After about a month, I could move normally again.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Did recovery cause much inconvenience in your daily life?

**Ms. Chen**

Of course. The garment held my arms slightly away from my body, so I could not lower them fully. It had to stay tightly wrapped, and I could not wear a bra during that time.

It has been nearly ten years since my operation, so I do not know how much the techniques have improved since then.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How did you manage work during recovery?

**Ms. Chen**

I scheduled the surgery during the National Day holiday. After the seven-day holiday, I could remove the garment. When I returned to work, I had to walk very slowly to avoid sweating.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Did the wound become inflamed during recovery?

**Ms. Chen**

No, but I understood that sweating during that period could increase the risk of inflammation.
