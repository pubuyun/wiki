**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What psychological pressures can underarm odor cause? Could people experience low self-esteem, anxiety, or excessive self-consciousness about their odor?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

There are two main aspects. First, many people do not understand underarm odor clearly. They may wonder whether it is a disease and feel inferior because they think they are different from others, especially when sweating. This can cause anxiety, shame, and even doubts about their health.

Second, they may worry that their odor affects other people and changes how others judge them. They may wonder whether people dislike them. Over time, they can lose confidence and avoid communication and social contact. Withdrawal can then reinforce itself in a cycle of increasing isolation.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What signs do you typically see in people experiencing psychological distress related to body odor?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

They may lack confidence, doubt themselves, and avoid interacting or communicating with others. They may become more withdrawn.

Their behavior can also change. They may avoid going out in summer or having contact with others. Even when walking, they may avoid moving their arms naturally because they worry about sweating, affecting other people, or being judged.

These concerns can gradually make them more isolated and less open, outgoing, and confident.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Which age group is most likely to experience psychological difficulties related to underarm odor, and why?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

Mainly adolescents going through puberty, roughly boys and girls aged 10 to 16.

One reason is physical development. In younger children, the underarm apocrine glands are not fully developed, so the odor is generally less noticeable. Around ages 15 or 16, as these glands mature, the clinical signs become more obvious.

Another reason is that ages 10 to 16 are important for developing self-awareness and personality. Their thinking and emotional development are not yet mature. Feeling different, or experiencing other people's reactions to their odor, can lead to psychological difficulties.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

You have already explained how this can affect social participation and daily life. Is there anything else you would add?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

Embarrassment or fear can also make people reluctant to seek medical help. Many do not seek care on their own.

Most of these patients come to us with their parents. They generally have two aims: to find out whether the odor can be eliminated at its source, and to seek help because they are already experiencing psychological difficulties.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Have you encountered patients whose psychological wellbeing was affected by underarm odor?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

Yes. However, we do not provide specialized psychological interventions for this group. We generally recommend that they consult a mental health specialist.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

We hope to develop a product that targets the odor-producing pathway and is gentler than products containing more irritating chemicals, including for adolescents. That is our development goal. Apart from effectiveness, what psychological considerations should inform its design?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

First, avoid treating underarm odor as a disease. In my view, it does not impair bodily functions or physiological health. It mainly involves odor arising from physical variation and development.

Body odor also varies between populations. I associate stronger body odor among Europeans and Americans with more developed sweat glands. The important message is that people may differ slightly in their development, and that this does not mean they have a disease. Understanding that can reduce fear.

Second, protect people's feelings in product design and promotion. Do not exaggerate the harm associated with underarm odor, especially psychological harm, because this could distress people or add to their burden.

Third, show respect and understanding. Avoid hurtful terms and use gentler, more tactful language.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Would it help to explain in our educational materials that this is a natural, genetically influenced characteristic rather than a sign of poor hygiene?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

Yes. Explain that it is a bodily variation, and make sure people know that there are ways to address it.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

That it can be managed?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

Yes. It can be managed, or addressed through medical approaches such as surgery. It is not necessarily unchangeable.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What other advice would you give us when communicating with people who have underarm odor?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

Be considerate. Some may be particularly sensitive or pay excessive attention to other people's reactions.

Avoid frowning, deliberately sniffing, or casually saying that there is an unpleasant smell. Pay attention to both your language and behavior.

You can reassure them that other people also experience this and that it is a common concern. Do not make them feel that you see them as unusual.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

So we should consider facial expressions and body language as well as words.

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

Exactly.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Should we include public education and psychological support in the project, explaining that underarm odor can be managed and is not simply a result of poor hygiene?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

Absolutely. In my view, underarm odor should not be categorized as a disease in itself. It does not seriously harm one's own or other people's physiological health; the odor mainly gives rise to psychological difficulties.

Beyond the effectiveness of the product, psychological support and education should therefore be a major part of your project's value.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Could we include that information in the product instructions, social media, and website?

**Dr. Zhou Lingen**

Yes, you should.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Based on your experience, what psychological pressures can underarm odor cause? Do patients experience low self-esteem, shame, anxiety, or repeated concerns about other people's judgments?

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

Many patients, especially children, do not understand underarm odor. This can lead to feelings of inferiority, shame, anxiety, and even excessive avoidance.

I understand the odor as being associated with more developed sweat glands. Everyone produces sweat during exercise. The secretions associated with underarm odor are initially odorless, but bacteria, including anaerobic bacteria, and local moisture contribute to an unpleasant smell.

With appropriate guidance, many young people can gradually address the issue and return to their usual social lives.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Which age groups have you observed experiencing these concerns most often?

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

Before 2000, when living conditions were generally poorer, I saw more patients from rural areas and adults over 30. Since then, I have increasingly seen people aged around 18, 19, or 20 actively seeking treatment.

In my experience, minimally invasive surgery can address 80% to 90% of the odor. A product like the one you propose could potentially help with what remains. I think underarm odor will become increasingly manageable or possible to eliminate.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Have you encountered patients who were reluctant to seek help because they felt embarrassed?

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

That is very common. Many children do not understand the condition, and they can also smell the odor themselves. Teasing or exclusion by classmates can lead to social anxiety, avoidance, and low self-esteem.

However, many now talk to their parents and actively seek care. Surgical methods have also changed. Instead of extensive sweat gland removal, we can use a smaller diamond-shaped excision and disrupt the follicles so that scar tissue forms, reducing the area removed while blocking the route to the skin surface.

By preventing these secretions from reaching external bacteria, the odor becomes much less noticeable. When I worked in a county hospital, these procedures were common, and people came from surrounding areas for treatment. Some also went to cosmetic surgery facilities. The results were generally satisfactory.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Apart from odor reduction, what experiences or needs matter to patients when using a deodorant product?

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

Their first concern is effectiveness. Even people with mild odor may want it eliminated. Everyone has some degree of sweat or body odor; the severity varies. It is important to help them avoid seeing themselves as fundamentally different from others.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Are discreet application and avoiding other people's attention important concerns?

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

I do not think those are the main issues. The important thing is to explain that people differ in their sweat gland activity and that there are ways to address the problem.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What language should we avoid in promotion or education to protect people's self-esteem?

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

You could say, “What can you do about underarm odor?” or “What can you do about unwanted odor?” and explain that help is available.

People with mild odor could use your product, while those with more severe odor could seek treatment at a hospital, with your product as a supplement. I think such a combination could eliminate more than 95% of the odor.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Should we include public education to help people understand underarm odor?

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

Yes. Education can help more young people understand the issue and reduce their psychological concerns. I think that would be very worthwhile.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Since you both have surgical experience, we would like to ask about compensatory sweating. We have seen accounts online of people sweating more in other areas after surgery. Have you encountered this clinically?

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

Under normal circumstances, I consider compensatory sweating elsewhere unlikely after blocking these secretion pathways.

Our physical surgical approach disrupts the follicles and tissue beneath the dermis. A diamond-shaped section, generally around 3 cm by 1.5 cm, is removed from the central area, which reduces the amount of secretion reaching the surface.

There is also a wider area of subcutaneous clearance. The skin remains, but scar tissue forms beneath the dermis, above the sweat glands. The aim is to block the outward passage of the secretions associated with underarm odor.

The reason I describe an 80% to 90% improvement is that the area treated is limited. Everyone sweats, and sweat can have an odor. The underarms have a higher concentration of glands and follicles, greater secretion, and bacterial activity that can make the odor stronger.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Our project focuses on a pathway involving ABCC11, which encodes a transporter that enables the apocrine glands to secrete an odor precursor. Underarm bacteria then metabolize that precursor into a distinctive odor molecule. That is the cause we are trying to address.

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

I think this problem will gradually become easier to resolve. You could consider how your product might complement surgery.

Traditional surgery removed a broad area of hair-bearing underarm skin. As understanding has improved, procedures have become less extensive and less traumatic, with smaller areas of skin removed and faster healing.

Some approaches also allow more complete access beneath the skin. However, I have not treated patients in recent years, so techniques may have advanced further. When I worked in a county hospital, particularly near schools, many parents brought students to consult me about surgery.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

So technological advances are increasing the possibility of addressing underarm odor more effectively.

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

Yes. I no longer see it as a major unresolved problem. Previously, limited access to care meant that many people, particularly in rural areas, did not seek treatment. The odor was more noticeable in hot weather and during sweating, which also explains why it was less noticeable in winter.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

For adolescents who are still developing and are not undergoing surgery, could products like ours help manage the issue?

**Dr. Hu Changshun**

For mild cases, such products could help cover the odor. Once people are over 18, surgery could in principle be considered, with your product as a supplement.

As I mentioned, surgery addresses around 80% to 90% of the problem, while some remaining odor may come from other glands and follicles. Sweating is necessary for regulating body temperature, so it is not possible to remove all the skin or eliminate sweating everywhere. Your product could have a supporting role in early, mild cases or other circumstances.
