**GB-SCIE26**
what is the formal medical name for genetically caused body odor? Is there a specific term for it?

**Doctor Xia-Yong Zhou**
Generally speaking, ordinary people call it 'fox odor' (body odor). In medicine, however,we usually just call it axillary osmidrosis. It is considered a skin condition. Strictlyspeaking, it has no real impact on physical health. It mainly causes social anxiety andsocial difficulties.

**GB-SCIE26**
Do different people experience it differently? For example, do age, sex, race, or ethnicityaffect how noticeable the odor is?

**Doctor Xia-Yong Zhou**
Yes, those factors do have an impact. For example, when we encounter Black people,their body odor is often much stronger. To be honest, almost all Black people have some
form of body odor, and nearly everyone uses scented products. From our perspective,the smell is not necessarily pleasant. It's not a natural fragrance; rather, it's body odormixed with perfume, which only masks the odor. Of course, White people and EastAsians can also have body odor. Differences among races definitely exist, but noteveryone is affected. It is simply more common among Black populations. Among EastAsians and White populations, some people have it and some do not.

**GB-SCIE26**
Among the patients you usually see, are there any differences between males andfemales? Or differences related to age?

**Doctor Xia-Yong Zhou**
We do see a lot of patients with body odor. I work in dermatology and cosmeticdermatology. From a clinical perspective, there doesn't seem to be a very obviousdifference. The youngest patient I saw recently was only four or five years old. I foundthat surprising because children that age have not really gone through puberty. Later,after examining the child, it was indeed present, although not very severe. On the otherhand, we also have patients in their forties and fifties who come for surgery. However,the majority are between fifteen and twenty-five years old. Of course, there are also quitea few patients in their thirties and forties. Overall, the age group from fifteen to thirty isthe most common. As for sex differences, I don't think there is any obvious distinction between males andfemales. It seems fairly random. Since it is hereditary, women can have it, men can haveit, and inheritance does not really depend on sex.

**GB-SCIE26**
When patients come for consultation, do you classify the severity of their body odor? Isthere a grading system?

**Doctor Xia-Yong Zhou**
Yes, there is definitely a grading system. Generally speaking, mild cases require veryclose proximity before the odor can be detected. Sometimes we have to smell veryclosely. In some cases, we use a cotton swab to collect sweat and smell it. That would beconsidered relatively mild. Moderate cases are different. Even slight movement canrelease noticeable odor. If you are sitting about one meter away, you may be able tosmell it. Severe cases are much more obvious. As soon as the person comes near you,the odor is clearly noticeable and sometimes quite pungent. That would definitely be classified as severe. Overall, assessment is mainly based on odor intensity, combinedwith medical history, such as whether parents or other family members also have thecondition. Generally, no special examinations are required. As doctors, we really do not have any specific physical signs to rely on.

**GB-SCIE26**
When patients come for consultation, do they commonly havemisconceptions about body odor?

Generally speaking, there are fewer misconceptions nowadays. Most people who cometo see a doctor care about the condition and actively seek information. We are now in theinformation age. People can search online through various platforms and learn about itthemselves. They often try different products, apply treatments on their own, and askwhether there are methods that can completely cure the condition.Compared with the past, people are much better informed. In the past, people mighthave relied on folk remedies. Nowadays, information is widely available, so I do not thinkthere are many major misconceptions.

here are quite a few products available. In China there are many traditional Chinesemedicine products, such as Xisilan Lotion or products marketed specifically for bodyodor. **GB-SCIE26**
What factors dopatients usually consider when choosing treatment? For example, cost or side effects?

**Doctor Xia-Yong Zhou**
The primary concern is the odor itself. Their main goal is to eliminate the smell. Many patients want a permanent solution so that they can live normally withoutembarrassment or discrimination from others. Once they want complete removal of the odor, surgery becomes the main option. Thenthey start considering things like whether the procedure can be scar-free or leave onlyminimal scarring. Ideally, they want no scars at all. Some patients also ask whether thereis a medication that can solve the problem without surgery.Besides surgery, we also use injections. There are treatments that intentionally cause inflammation and closure of sweat glands or their ducts. The goal is to make them closeoff so that sweat secretion decreases. We also use botulinum toxin injections. The principle is that botulinum toxin reducessweating by blocking nerve signals. Sweat is still produced, but it cannot be released as easily. Since body odor develops after sweat is broken down by bacteria on the skin surface,reducing sweat release can reduce odor. There are also microneedle treatments and microwave-based treatments. Microwave treatment is completely non-invasive. It uses microwave energy to heat thetarget tissue to around 60°C, destroying the apocrine glands while preserving the skinsurface. Of course, damaging the skin surface is not acceptable. Microneedle treatment works on a similar principle by delivering heat beneath the skinsurface. However, it is minimally invasive because needles are inserted into the skin. These are among the most expensive options. Microwave treatment can cost twenty tothirty thousand RMB. Microneedle treatment may cost around ten thousand RMB. Then there are surgical methods. Nowadays we try to keep incisions as small as possible. However, I often tell patients that they need to decide what matters most to them. If they prioritize complete removal ofodor, larger procedures may be necessary. If they prioritize avoiding scars, then less invasive options may be more appropriate. Some minimally invasive procedures involve making a small incision and using specialized tools to scrape or remove tissue underneath the skin. Some surgeons claim they can completely remove the glands through a tiny incision.Personally, I think it is difficult to guarantee complete removal when visibility is limited.There are also endoscopic approaches that allow visualization through a small opening,but even then it is not always easy to remove everything completely.Each method has its own advantages and disadvantages.Some treatments can produce good short-term results but may not be permanent. Even when glands are blocked, they can sometimes reopen over time.
Botulinum toxin is mainly a short-term solution. Its effects usually last about three to fourmonths before gradually wearing off.As for various products and topical treatments, I am actually less familiar with those than with medical procedures

**GB-SCIE26**
From a doctor's perspective, do you think our product has any advantages compared with existing options? Are there any areas for improvement or potential risks?

**Doctor Xia-Yong Zhou**
In my opinion, botulinum toxin injections are very expensive and only last about three orfour months. Your product would be quite different because botulinum toxin requires injections. Many people are afraid of injections. You’re still students, but imagine having to receivemultiple injections in different spots under the arm. Even if someone is brave, they maystill dislike it. If your product could achieve a similar effect without injections, then itwould have a clear advantage.Whether it can actually achieve that effect is another question. I think that may bechallenging. The biggest advantage is that it is non-invasive and externally applied. There is no fearassociated with it. This is especially important for younger patients. Some children develop severe body odor as early as twelve or thirteen years old because of genetics. At that age, surgery is generally not very suitable. We usually recommend surgery after the age of seventeen because younger individuals may not have completed development yet. If development is not complete, even if you remove the glands, new development maystill occur later. For these younger patients, topical products are much more suitable. They are oftenafraid of injections because treatment may require many injection points under the arm. So the advantages are obvious. The biggest concern is whether the product will beeffective enough. Since it is a protein, it may be degraded, washed away, or affected bythe external environment. The key question is whether it can achieve an effectcomparable to injections. If it can, then I think it would be an excellent product.

**GB-SCIE26**
Besidessweat secretion, are there other aspects of the underarm environment that could affectits efficacy or stability?

**Doctor Xia-Yong Zhou**
Definitely. The physiological environment varies from person to person. Some peoplehave more acidic skin, while others are less acidic. Humidity is also important. Some people sweat heavily, and different clothing habitsaffect temperature and moisture levels. All these factors can influence protein stability. or example, high temperatures can denature proteins. We know proteins become unstable when temperatures rise above physiological levels.Increased sweating can also dilute the product or wash it away. As a result, the effective concentration of the product may decrease, which wouldcertainly affect efficacy.

In addition, issues such as cytotoxicity and long-term safety should be evaluated.For a protein product, you should also consider whether it could be absorbed by thebody and whether it might cause effects on organs such as the liver or kidneys, eventhough absorption is likely to be minimal.These are all aspects that should be considered during development and whencommunicating with users.
