**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What are the greatest threats to coastal and marine ecosystems?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

In my view, the greatest threat is disturbance from human activities. This includes pollutants and toxic substances that disrupt ecosystems, as well as the encroachment on habitats that interferes with organisms' normal physiological activities.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Compared with other pollution sources, what are the characteristics of personal care ingredients in terms of ecological toxicity and environmental persistence? These products may enter the sea directly from people's skin or through wastewater.

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

Personal care products contain complex mixtures of chemicals with different properties. One important concern is endocrine disruption. Some substances have estrogen-like or androgen-like effects and may interfere with marine organisms' endocrine systems, potentially affecting populations.

Some of these chemicals are persistent and difficult to degrade. Bioaccumulation and amplification through biological transfer can also be concerns, even if concentrations in the environment or individual organisms are initially low.

Current surveys do not necessarily provide enough evidence to establish that harm has already occurred. Some of these effects remain potential risks.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How can discarded plastic packaging and associated chemicals affect marine ecosystems?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

Sunlight, water, and other environmental conditions cause discarded packaging to age and fragment into smaller pieces, producing microplastics. I have not personally surveyed microplastic concentrations, but the literature and reporting indicate that marine microplastic pollution is a major concern.

An important role of microplastics is that they can act as carriers for pollutants through adsorption. Their sizes can resemble those of natural food particles, such as algae or zooplankton, so marine animals may ingest them by mistake.

This can interfere with growth and nutrition. An organism may feel full despite having eaten material that provides no nutrition. The pollutants carried on the particles can also have effects, including endocrine disruption. Particles retained against the intestinal wall may cause inflammation and other intestinal problems. Together, these can threaten the organism's health.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Which categories of personal care ingredients are particularly concerning from a marine toxicology perspective?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

Organic pollutants are an important concern. These include plasticizers, UV-filter ingredients in sunscreens, and antimicrobial or preservative ingredients. Phthalates are one example of a concerning group of chemicals.

Some sunscreen ingredients have been reported to affect coral reefs, interfere with growth and reproduction, and cause cellular toxicity. Preservative and antimicrobial ingredients can also act as endocrine disruptors, affecting reproductive and immune systems.

Another category is microplastics, which can present both physical and chemical threats.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Conventional underarm products may contain aluminum compounds, synthetic fragrances, and preservatives. Have such ingredients been detected or shown to harm marine organisms after entering water?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

Aluminum compounds have potential toxicity, although excessive aluminum concentrations have not been a prominent finding in the coastal surveys I am familiar with.

For preservatives and disinfectants such as triclosan, field risk assessments do not always establish clear effects. However, laboratory studies involving long-term exposure at environmental concentrations, including experiments lasting around six months, have found toxicity in model organisms. Endocrine disruption is one of the clearest concerns.

However, dose matters. Discussing toxicity without considering dose is not meaningful. At appropriate exposure levels, the risks may be acceptable, since completely avoiding all such substances is not realistic.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How do personal care chemicals reach the sea, and can wastewater treatment remove them?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

The main route is domestic wastewater. Products enter drains, pass through wastewater treatment plants, and are discharged into rivers that eventually reach the sea.

Most treatment plants are not designed to remove every trace organic pollutant of this kind. Effluent can meet discharge standards while still containing some of these substances.

Direct inputs can also occur at beaches and heavily visited coastal tourist areas, although I consider these a smaller pathway.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

If we use recyclable or biodegradable packaging instead of conventional plastic, can we assume it is safe for marine ecosystems?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

No, safety cannot be guaranteed. Degradable plastics and even paper-based products may still contain chemical additives. They may have a smaller impact, but they can still carry substances into the environment. They may be better options, but that is not an absolute guarantee.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

If we want to develop a more sustainable personal care product, are there particular substances we should avoid?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

I do not know enough about personal care formulations to identify specific ingredients as relatively safe. From the perspective of sunscreens, I would generally expect physical protection approaches to have a smaller subsequent environmental impact than chemical sunscreen ingredients. That is the extent of the advice I can offer on this question.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Our product does not contain sunscreen. Its active ingredient would be a synthetic protein applied to the underarms.

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

If it is a protein, I would expect a smaller impact because it can biodegrade. After it is washed off during bathing and enters water, I would expect it to break down.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Could ingestion by marine organisms cause harm? Could a high concentration of the artificial protein stimulate excessive growth and lead to oxygen depletion?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

I do not think your product would reach that scale of impact. Its user base would be limited, and the amount used would not be particularly large, so I would expect the impact to be controllable.

I do not know the exact protein you are designing, but I would expect a synthetic protein to degrade gradually in the environment. I consider it unlikely that the quantities reaching the sea would cause excessive growth. Dilution would also reduce the concentration substantially.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What tests or certifications could help demonstrate that our protein is environmentally friendly, particularly toward the marine environment?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

You could measure its degradation rate after use. For example, simulate what happens in natural water, which contains microorganisms capable of breaking down proteins.

Use natural water in an experiment and observe how quickly the protein degrades. Rapid degradation would be reassuring. You could also assess its effects on model organisms commonly used in ecotoxicology to see whether it causes significant harm.

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

Law is not my area of expertise. There are restrictions relating to issues such as microplastics, but I am not aware of a dedicated Chinese law specifically addressing personal care products in this context.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Are there real-world examples of personal care chemicals affecting a particular marine area?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

You could look into the effects of sunscreen products on coral reef ecosystems. I remember reports suggesting that sunscreens threatened reefs and contributed to coral bleaching, particularly in some coastal countries.

I have not seen a similarly prominent, widely reported domestic case involving personal care products and marine ecosystems. The examples I remember were overseas.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What are the main pollution pressures in the Pearl River Delta and nearby coastal waters?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

One of the largest pressures is nutrient input from land. Nutrients such as inorganic nitrogen and phosphates can be elevated, particularly in waters closer to the inner Pearl River Delta. Some areas exceed Class III limits.

There can also be excessive concentrations of metals such as copper and zinc, especially during the dry season.

Other pollutants, including plasticizers, microplastics, and flame retardants, present potential risks. Rather than necessarily describing them as exceeding a standard, researchers survey their concentrations and assess the risks. Many assessments identify moderate risks, with higher risks in some locations.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Are there monitoring data for these other chemicals in the Pearl River Estuary or farther into the South China Sea?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

Yes. There have been extensive surveys of substances such as flame retardants, including organophosphate flame retardants. Some provinces also conduct baseline marine pollution surveys.

There are monitoring data for metals associated with activities such as electronic waste dismantling as well.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Are coastal cities taking specific measures to address microplastics or pollution from consumer chemicals?

**Ms. Zhang Linbao**

I am not aware of many specific measures at present. Much of the effort involves public messaging encouraging people to reduce plastic use.

Hainan is one example where a plastics ban has been introduced. My understanding is that it requires degradable plastic products in place of non-degradable ones, although some prohibited products are still used. Environmental policies are gradually catching up.
