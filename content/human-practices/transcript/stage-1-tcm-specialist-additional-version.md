**GB-SCIE26**
What are the common Traditional Chinese Medicine treatments for axillary odor currently used in clinical practice?

**Mr. Hu Zhenhua**
In Traditional Chinese Medicine, there are mainly two approaches to treating body odor, or axillary odor. Simply speaking, there are two methods. One is oral medication, such as Chinese herbal decoctions or pills, which are taken internally. The other is external application, using topical Traditional Chinese Medicine treatments.

For internal treatment, Traditional Chinese Medicine generally believes that this condition is related to liver fire. More specifically, it is considered to be caused by stagnation of liver qi transforming into heat.

So, for this kind of problem, which is understood as liver qi stagnation with heat, Traditional Chinese Medicine would use Longdan Xiegan Decoction. This prescription is used to clear liver fire. In TCM theory, damp-heat is caused by liver qi leading to internal heat. This is the Traditional Chinese Medicine understanding of axillary odor.

For external treatment, it mainly involves several toxic medicinal substances. You may also be able to find these online. One is called Mituoseng, and another is Qingfen. These are the main ones used. In addition, alum is also used. These substances are applied externally.

Other herbs may also be added. For example, soap pod, or Zaojiao. In ancient times, soap pod was used like soap. People would boil soap pod in water and use it to clean the armpit area. Then, the medicines would be ground into powder, including Mituoseng, Qingfen, and some other Chinese medicinal ingredients. These are the main ingredients. The powder would then be applied to the armpit area, and over time, it may have some effect.

There is also another method using hot cooked rice. Since rice is hot after being cooked, the Chinese medicine can be placed inside the rice and then held under the armpit. This may also have a certain effect.

So, in Traditional Chinese Medicine, these are mainly the two approaches: internal oral treatment and external topical treatment.

**GB-SCIE26**
Through what mechanisms do these herbs or treatment methods work? For example, do they work through antibacterial effects?

**Mr. Hu Zhenhua**
As for the mechanisms, internal medicine mainly works by regulating the body's metabolism. In modern terms, we can say it regulates metabolism. For example, Longdan Xiegan Decoction contains around ten Chinese medicinal herbs. Its main function is to clear liver fire. In TCM theory, the liver and gallbladder are internally and externally related, so liver fire and gallbladder fire are connected. Longdan Xiegan Decoction clears heat. To remove dampness, it also includes ingredients that promote water metabolism. In modern terms, this means promoting urination. Dampness is expelled through urination. This is the mechanism of internal treatment.

From a modern perspective, the mechanism of external treatment may be that it damages part of the sweat glands. These are toxic medicines. As I mentioned earlier, Mituoseng is lead oxide, a lead-containing compound. Qingfen in Traditional Chinese Medicine is mercurous chloride. These two substances can have a certain damaging effect on sweat glands.

The active component of alum is hydrated potassium aluminium sulfate. Its main function is probably to reduce sweating. When sweating is reduced, the secretion related to axillary odor may also decrease. It may also help shrink the pores. That is roughly the idea.

**GB-SCIE26**
What are the side effects of these medicines?

**Mr. Hu Zhenhua**
From today's perspective, Traditional Chinese Medicine is not particularly effective in treating axillary odor. The medicines I just mentioned need to be used repeatedly. Nowadays, since people are more aware that some of these medicines are toxic and that the dosage is difficult to control, Traditional Chinese Medicine methods are used relatively less often. Other methods may include some dermatological medicines with lower toxicity. For example, Baixianpi and Baifuzi may be used.

Yes, these medicines all have side effects. They are all toxic. The first issue is that the dosage is very difficult to control. A small dose may not cause problems, because many cosmetics also contain similar substances. For example, around the Dragon Boat Festival, when people make preserved eggs, Mituoseng is used, which contains lead. In the past, cosmetics mainly used Mituoseng, a lead-containing substance. Because lead-containing substances can make the face look shiny, they certainly have an effect. However, the dosage is very difficult to control. If the dosage is too high, side effects may occur.

Nowadays, for axillary odor, most Traditional Chinese Medicine practitioners would suggest surgery or treatments that destroy the sweat glands. If a patient comes for treatment now, we may generally prescribe some internal oral medicine. After taking it, the degree of odor may sometimes be reduced slightly.

**GB-SCIE26**
How much does a complete course of treatment usually cost?

**Mr. Hu Zhenhua**
For something like Longdan Xiegan Decoction, or pills such as Danggui Longhui Pills, if it is prepared as a decoction, it may cost around 100 yuan per dose. Generally speaking, the treatment course is about three weeks. If it is in pill form, it may cost around 30 yuan per day, and the patient may take it for about one month.

**GB-SCIE26**
In your opinion, how familiar is the Traditional Chinese Medicine field with synthetic biology?

**Mr. Hu Zhenhua**
In theory, Traditional Chinese Medicine and synthetic biology belong to two different academic systems, so the TCM field is relatively less familiar with synthetic biology. However, in the area of Chinese medicinal chemistry, some people are currently working on related issues. For example, the substances I mentioned earlier, such as Mituoseng and Qingfen, and these treatment methods, are also being studied by many Traditional Chinese Medicine universities. They are studying how to use these medicines effectively through modern methods, including how to standardize and control dosage. However, I have not yet seen large-scale clinical reports.

So there are still many active ingredients in Chinese medicinal herbs?

**Mr. Hu Zhenhua**
There are many methods. There are many methods, but they are used relatively less often nowadays. They are not commonly used. As I said earlier, the main indispensable substances are still Mituoseng, Qingfen, and alum, which are inorganic substances. But there are also many unusual formulas. There are quite a lot of methods for treating the armpit area. If you search on Baidu or other platforms now, most of the information you find will still mainly involve these substances.
