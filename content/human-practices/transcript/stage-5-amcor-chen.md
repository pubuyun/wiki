**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

When enterprise clients bring a brand-new product to you, what dimensions do you normally prioritize for recommending packaging solutions, such as cost, product protection, environmental-friendliness and user experience?

**Ms. Chen**

First of all, product protection performance comes absolutely first, including oxygen-barrier, water-barrier, puncture-resistance and heat-sealing performance. We cannot talk about sustainability without guaranteeing basic product protection. Then we select suitable sustainability pathways based on each client’s actual requirements. Common available options include single-material recyclable packaging, paper-based recyclable packaging, PCR post-consumer recycled-material packaging, and optimizing by shrinking overall packaging dimensions. Next we look at end-manufacturing costs. A major pain-point for sustainable packaging right now is the frequent mismatch between environmental performance and real-world cost. We also need to check compatibility with the client’s existing production lines. Many eco-friendly packaging concepts look great on paper but run into huge practical obstacles during mass production, so manufacturability cannot be overlooked. Consumer habits and end-user experience also weigh heavily. Even the most sustainable design will fail in the market if customers find it awkward or inconvenient to use. Regional environmental regulations are another key factor, since requirements differ widely from country to country and region to region. Last but not least, we assess compatibility with end-of-life waste-recovery systems. Domestic recycling infrastructure in China still has many gaps. Even well-designed recyclable packaging delivers little environmental benefit if local collection and sorting systems cannot process it properly.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Are there relevant domestic environmental-protection policies for packaging in China?

**Ms. Chen**

There are multiple relevant policies already in place. Hainan’s well-known plastic-ban regulation is one representative example. That being said, China still lacks one unified over-arching national regulation covering the whole packaging sector. Different industries operate under separate sets of rules. Within the plastics industry, national strategies target carbon-neutrality and plastic-pollution reduction, but binding mandatory requirements have not been rolled-out nationwide. European regulatory standards are far stricter in this area. The EU PPWR (Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation) sets comprehensive binding rules for packaging sustainability, recyclability, recycled-content quotas and reuse-refill targets for all member states.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Is the industry continuously researching and rolling-out more sustainable packaging materials?

**Ms. Chen**

Yes, R&D and commercial promotion of sustainable packaging materials remain very active across the industry. Three major technical directions stand out. First is single-material recyclable packaging, mostly all-PE or all-PP constructions that do not require multi-layer separation at the recycling stage. In the Chinese market, MDO-PE (machine-direction oriented PE) combined with pure-PE structures are gaining traction, delivering fully-recyclable packaging solutions. The second direction is paper-based packaging, usually with paper content above 80 % plus functional auxiliary layers for product protection. One well-known real-world case is Amcor China’s paper-based packaging for M&M’s. Bio-based PLA material is integrated into the structure, making the whole package biodegradable after consumer use. Third is PCR post-consumer recycled plastic. It is important to note regional regulatory differences. Domestic Chinese rules restrict PCR materials for food-contact scenarios, while permitting them for cosmetic and personal-care goods. Some overseas markets such as Australia allow PCR materials even for food-contact packaging.

From your company’s observation, have corporate demands for eco-friendly packaging increased? What are their top concerns?

**Ms. Chen**

Before the pandemic, large multinational brands including PepsiCo and Unilever published public sustainability roadmaps, and market enthusiasm for green packaging ran very high. However the economic downturn after the pandemic shifted priorities. While companies still value sustainability, cost-performance has moved higher up the agenda. Many enterprises have pushed their original 2025 sustainability targets back to 2030. Compared with multinational corporations, local Chinese enterprises feel less urgent pressure to adopt green packaging. Most domestic brands only act when forced by regulatory compliance requirements, and they keep cost-balance firmly in mind for every sustainability-related change.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

So public and corporate focus on sustainable packaging is heavily linked to overall economic conditions?

**Ms. Chen**

Exactly. Right now most companies’ core priority is driving business growth. When overall consumption weakens in an economic downturn, enterprises focus heavily on cost-cutting and efficiency gains. Sustainability remains a popular public topic, yet it is often not the top business priority.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What are the biggest barriers preventing wider adoption of sustainable-packaging materials in China?

**Ms. Chen**

There are three main obstacles. First, without strong nationwide mandatory regulatory requirements, large-scale market roll-out faces natural resistance. Voluntary sustainability initiatives can only go so far. Second, economic pressure makes cost a major sticking-point for most clients. Many green material options carry higher unit costs compared with conventional alternatives. Third, businesses need to carefully evaluate real-world commercial returns. Companies hesitate to switch unless they can confirm that sustainable packaging will actually boost end-consumer purchasing willingness, rather than merely adding expense.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Which eco-packaging technologies or materials hold the strongest future-development potential in your view?

**Ms. Chen**

Every sustainability pathway has its own strengths and applicable scenarios. There is no universal “best” material. The most suitable solution always depends on the client’s product features, budget, target-consumer groups, production-line setup and local waste-management infrastructure. Some projects perform best with single-material plastic; others achieve better results adopting paper-based structures or refill-oriented designs. We evaluate each project case-by-case rather than recommending one-size-fits-all material choices.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What are the easily-overlooked yet critical issues for companies wanting to adopt sustainable packaging?

**Ms. Chen**

Quite a few clients focus only on material technical specs while ignoring full-life-cycle carbon footprint, including emissions generated during raw-material manufacturing, logistics transportation and end-of-life disposal. Consumer education is another commonly-neglected point. Even well-designed recyclable packaging delivers limited value if end-users do not understand how to sort and dispose of it correctly. Supporting downstream recycling infrastructure also matters a great deal. Several large multinational corporations are now investing resources to help improve local collection-and-sorting systems to match their sustainable-packaging roll-outs. Some clients chase green credentials but sacrifice essential product-protection performance, which ultimately damages product quality and user experience. Compatibility with existing factory production lines is frequently underestimated. Certain eco-materials cannot run smoothly on existing filling or sealing equipment and require costly production-line modification. Last, regional regulatory differences must always be checked in advance. Packaging that meets standards in one region may violate rules in another target market.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

In real-world practical application, do refill-style packaging systems deliver the full environmental benefits people expect? What factors can weaken their actual sustainability performance?

**Ms. Chen**

When properly implemented, refill-based systems can deliver substantial environmental gains. For example, in shampoo and laundry-detergent categories we produce stand-up flexible refill pouches. Consumers keep and reuse their original rigid outer bottle and top-up contents from the lightweight pouch. Flexible refill pouches use far less plastic material compared with producing a brand-new rigid bottle for every purchase. Their light weight also cuts carbon emissions across manufacturing and transportation links. We can further boost sustainability by building pouches from fully single-material recyclable constructions. In China, refill-packaging solutions have seen the widest real-world adoption within the home-and-personal-care sector. That said, several factors can erode theoretical environmental advantages. If consumers throw away the durable main container instead of reusing it repeatedly, most refill-system benefits vanish. Poor-quality refill-pouch designs that use multi-layer composite non-recyclable materials simply shift waste problems rather than solving them. User experience also counts; clumsy, leak-prone refill-pouch designs will make consumers reject the whole concept.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

For protein-based formulations, do roll-on ball applicator packages create special requirements for packaging materials and structural design?

**Ms. Chen**

We first identify key risks that the protein-rich contents bring to packaging systems. Major concerns include protein adsorption onto inner surfaces, oxidative degradation, microbial growth, and pH-value effects from the formulation. Protein ingredients tend to stick to package inner walls. Therefore we need to assess inner-surface adsorption behavior carefully. We may select low-adsorption base substrates, or apply tailored hydrophobic or hydrophilic inner coatings to minimize protein residue, phase-separation and unpleasant odor build-up over shelf-life. Oxidation risks demand high oxygen-barrier and moisture-barrier packaging performance. Ordinary PP material will not suffice. We can consider single-PP substrates combined with specialized barrier coatings; professional packaging engineers need to specify exact coating grades. Material properties that suppress microbial proliferation also require careful technical review by packaging specialists. Roll-on ball structures commonly trap residual liquid inside the assembly. Design adjustments such as optimized ball-fit clearance and extra-smooth surface finishes can reduce trapped leftover product. The pH of your protein formulation must align with substrate chemical resistance. Different materials perform very differently under acidic versus alkaline conditions. PCR recycled plastics are widely used within cosmetic-and-personal-care packaging and represent a valid option if you want to highlight green-product credentials.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Can adding a hydrophilic inner coating solve protein-adsorption problems on hydrophobic plastic roll-on-package inner walls?

**Ms. Chen**

This is a highly technical material-interface question. I would recommend you consult dedicated packaging-material specialists for authoritative testing-backed answers, rather than relying only on theoretical assumptions. Coating compatibility with your specific protein formulation, coating durability during shelf-life, and interactions with other product additives all need practical lab validation.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

If we adopt single-material PP construction to improve roll-on-ball package recyclability, will that compromise product-protection performance?

**Ms. Chen**

Note that PE is more typically deployed for flexible soft-packaging applications. Pure unmodified single-PP provides very limited oxygen-barrier performance. If your protein-containing formulation requires oxidation protection, simple all-PP without additional barrier treatments will not satisfy your shelf-life requirements. You would need to integrate silicon-oxide sputtered barrier layers onto your single-PP base to achieve sufficient gas-blocking capability. Every barrier modification must also not break the overall recyclability of your single-material design.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Comparing refill pouches and roll-on-ball hard-container formats, what are the typical differences in production-related costs?

**Ms. Chen**

Roll-on ball packaging sits at the higher-cost end. Tooling-mold expenses are substantial because roll-on assemblies are custom-designed parts; custom mold development can range from several hundred-thousand up to over a million RMB. Only extremely-large-volume production runs can effectively amortize these high upfront mold-investment costs. Additional complex production-assembly steps further lift manufacturing expenditure. Logistical costs are also higher: rigid roll-on bottles take-up much more space during pallet stacking and transport compared with compressed flexible pouches. By contrast refill-style flexible pouches deliver significant cost advantages. There is no expensive custom-mold capital outlay. Total plastic-material consumption per-unit is low. Transport efficiency improves dramatically; far more refill pouches fit inside the same shipping container or rail carriage, lowering per-unit logistics expense greatly. The main commercial downside for flexible refill-packaging lies in retail-shelf presentation. Consumers often perceive soft pouches as less premium-looking compared with rigid bottles. Japanese markets widely adopt beautifully-designed high-end flexible refill packaging. Within the Chinese market, most brands combine both rigid primary containers and soft refill-pouch SKUs to accommodate mainstream consumer aesthetic expectations.
