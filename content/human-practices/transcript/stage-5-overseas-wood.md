**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Thank you for taking the time to participate in this interview. We are the SCIE iGEM team, participating in the International Genetically Engineered Machine Competition. Our project aims to develop a gentle, targeted solution to underarm odor.

Through this interview, we hope to better understand how underarm odor is perceived and managed in different cultural contexts. Your perspective will inform the human practices and product development aspects of our project. We understand that body odor can be a personal topic, so please share only what you feel comfortable discussing.

May we take notes and record this interview for our project? With your permission, we may also include parts of the interview on our team wiki.

**Mr. Wood**

Yes.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Which country or cultural environment did you mainly grow up in?

**Mr. Wood**

Mainly the UK. I would say my background is more British than anything else. I spent about half my life in Britain and the other half living in different places, including the Middle East, China, and Korea.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

In your culture or community, how is underarm odor generally perceived?

**Mr. Wood**

Generally, it is not perceived positively. People with underarm odor are often seen as unhygienic or dirty.

There is some understanding that people may have been working or doing something that makes them sweat. Even so, it is generally viewed negatively. Even in a gym, people may feel that there is something you could do about it.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Do people with noticeable underarm odor experience stigma, social exclusion, or unfair treatment?

**Mr. Wood**

In the UK, I think it is relatively rare to encounter someone with a noticeable underarm odor problem. People may experience stigma and name-calling at an early age, and I think most probably learn to do something about it.

It is unusual to notice it in adults. If someone is homeless, for example, people may understand that buying deodorant is not their highest priority. Generally, though, people manage it, perhaps partly because of the stigma they experienced when they were younger.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What factors have shaped your perception of underarm odor, such as family, friends, or cultural norms?

**Mr. Wood**

Probably cultural norms, along with family and friends. Your family will tell you if there is a problem. Your mother might buy you deodorant or antiperspirant.

Friends may point it out less politely. Other people might not tell you directly, but they may make comments or avoid sitting next to you. Usually, family and friends will let you know, although some will be more polite about it than others.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Have your views on underarm odor changed over time?

**Mr. Wood**

Not really, no.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Have you noticed differences in attitudes toward body odor between your culture and other cultures?

**Mr. Wood**

In some traditional communities in parts of Africa, particularly among older men, I have encountered the view that body odor is masculine or a sign of being a “real man.” Some men see it positively, although women may not share that view.

I would say that this is changing among younger men. It is more something I associate with the older generation.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Do people around you generally share similar views on body odor, or do their opinions differ?

**Mr. Wood**

Generally, they have similar views. That may be because many people in my circle of friends have backgrounds similar to mine. However, I also know people from Africa and other places, and they generally share the same attitude.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Do you currently use products to manage underarm odor?

**Mr. Wood**

Yes. Apart from washing, I tend to use deodorant rather than antiperspirant. Antiperspirants reduce sweating, which I do not particularly like because the body is designed to sweat. I prefer something that controls the smell rather than stops the sweating.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What do you like or dislike about your current deodorant or antiperspirant products? What could be improved?

**Mr. Wood**

They seem very expensive in China, so I usually bring deodorant from the UK. I do not bring many things from the UK, but deodorant is one of them. Perhaps I have not looked carefully enough for local options, but it seems to be one of the few things that costs more in China than in the UK.

I also dislike constantly throwing away the cans. I do not think that is good for the environment. I keep thinking that I should look into refillable options.

I hope that most products no longer contain CFCs, which they used to contain.

I prefer sprays to roll-ons because they feel more refreshing and provide wider coverage. If you feel that you smell, you can cover more of your body with a spray. However, a liquid product might be better for the environment, so perhaps I should use that more.
