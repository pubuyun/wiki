**Ms. Sefe**

I grew up in the United Kingdom, particularly in Wimbledon in southwest London.

Thinking back to my teenage years, there was quite a lot of body odor because our bodies were changing. I remember boys who played a lot of sports coming back to lessons smelling strongly. We certainly did not view body odor positively.

If young people came to school with noticeable odor even though they had not been playing sports, they were stigmatized. It implied that they did not have a good hygiene routine or were not being looked after at home. There was a strong negative stigma attached to body odor.

I think this continues into adulthood, especially if the odor is persistent. People may interpret it as a sign that someone does not take care of themselves, or that something is wrong and they are not paying attention to their personal care.

**Ms. Sefe**

Within the family, parents are often the first people to remind you to be clean and smell pleasant. I remember my mother insisting that I brush my teeth again before leaving the house.

I also remember seeing many short advertisements when I was younger that suggested women needed the right deodorant to feel confident in public. The message was that nervousness would make you sweat more and produce odor, and that buying the product would prevent that problem.

Now that I am older and have learned more about the chemistry of deodorants and antiperspirants, I am more selective about what I buy. I avoid certain ingredients because I am concerned about possible long-term health effects. My concern comes from studies I understand to have reported correlations between some deodorants or antiperspirants and cancers involving the lymphatic system.

**Ms. Sefe**

Across the different cultures I have encountered, I have noticed a similar attitude: people generally do not like body odor. I have traveled a lot, and that has been my observation wherever I have been, including among people from different social backgrounds within a country.

**Ms. Sefe**

I really like The Ordinary's glycolic acid because it has multiple uses. You can use it on your face, arms, and other parts of the body. That is convenient, especially when traveling, because one small container can serve several purposes. It is my main go-to product.

At night, I use glycolic acid to remove the day's makeup and dirt, then shower before bed. I also use it for a quick application the following morning. I use Native at the beginning of the day after showering.
