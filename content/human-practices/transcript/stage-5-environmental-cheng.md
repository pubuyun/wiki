**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

First, regarding current packaging issues, we would like to understand what are the main environmental problems currently faced by plastic packaging?

**Mr. Cheng**

Plastic packaging is a well-known global issue, and its environmental impacts are mainly reflected in several aspects. The first is the degradation problem.

Because plastic is used in extremely large quantities, everyone is paying attention to its degradation issue. Even after recycling, the utilization rate is not very high. Some parts can be recycled, such as certain bottles and other items that people often recycle. However, the parts that cannot be recycled face many environmental problems during disposal. If they are not properly recycled, they will be discarded everywhere. Therefore, regarding the degradation of plastic materials themselves, the degradation period is usually several hundred years, or even more than 500 years. In addition, even the treatment methods are not ideal. Many are still disposed of through landfills. Landfills actually require a very long time for degradation and also occupy space.

If incineration is used more frequently, everyone knows that incineration produces a large amount of toxic gases and so on.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Compared with plastic packaging, what are the advantages and disadvantages of aluminum packaging that is popular on the market?

**Mr. Cheng**

The price of aluminum is relatively high, but aluminum is the most abundant metal element on Earth, second only to oxygen, silicon, and iron. However, processing aluminum is an industry with very high energy consumption. Despite this, aluminum has many advantages compared with other packaging materials. Among packaging materials, aluminum is widely used because it has relatively good oxygen barrier properties.

Once a product is isolated from oxygen, it receives additional protection. Most packaging materials only block light, while aluminum can block both light and oxygen. Therefore, it can prevent products from being oxidized or protect products for a longer period of time. Another advantage of aluminum is that it is easy to recycle, especially because most aluminum materials can be recycled individually. For example, the aluminum material used for drinking Coca-Cola cans is very easy to reuse.

From the perspective of aluminum, there are many types of plastic packaging. Nowadays, more and more packaging uses single-material plastics, but non-single-material plastics can theoretically be recycled, although they have major problems. For example, recycling is more difficult because many people believe that complex plastics, such as non-single-material plastic bottles, may not be recyclable. Even if they are recycled, packaging like Tetra Pak cartons, because they contain paper, aluminum, and PE, cannot be directly reused after recycling and can only be used in downgraded applications.

Downgraded use is not very environmentally friendly to some extent. Although downgraded use has an environmentally friendly aspect, recycled materials are difficult to separate and process, such as PE, which is difficult to break down.

The production processes of aluminum, paper, and other materials themselves consume a lot of energy, and there is also loss during the process. Therefore, from the perspective of cost and environmental protection, plastic packaging or mixed packaging forms are not ideal. If cost factors are excluded, aluminum packaging still has many advantages. Of course, from the perspective of packaging properties, aluminum packaging also has some problems. For example, many aluminum packaging materials are prone to becoming brittle.

However, once damaged, it will affect the appearance. In addition, if the thickness is inappropriate, it may crack, because compared with plastic, aluminum does not have as much flexibility. Therefore, during many packaging material production processes, there is a specialized inspection device used to detect whether aluminum materials have very small pinholes. Large defects can usually be seen, but these small defects may not be noticed during packaging.

Ultimately, this can lead to product quality problems.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

I understand. When you mentioned single-material plastic packaging, do you mean that the material itself is single? For example, packaging made entirely of PE?

**Mr. Cheng**

Although they are all plastics, PE itself can also be divided into many types, right? The most ideal situation is to use one specific plastic material or one specific type of PE. However, many times adhesives are needed, right?

They are used to bond different types of plastic materials together. Even if some products look like plastic, they are actually different amounts of plastic bonded together to achieve certain functions. Because as packaging, it needs to protect the contents, provide oxygen resistance, light resistance, and also have a certain load-bearing capacity, etc. Right?

It also needs to look attractive, and the color cannot be too unattractive, etc. Therefore, many chemical substances are combined together. It is a combination of multiple materials. Aluminum, on the other hand, is relatively easy to use as a single material. However, during transportation or packaging, unless it is packaged very well, damage can still occur.

I believe this is the most important reason. As long as there is commercial benefit.

We live in a commercial society. As long as the commercial benefits are sufficient, the entire business chain will operate. This is the biggest reason: cost. Of course, as regulations continue to strengthen, corporate restrictions are also increasing. In addition, when technology is gradually understood by people, more people will be willing to pay a premium for it. As acceptance increases, companies will also be willing to invest more. This is somewhat like a positive cycle. When companies can make profits from it, they will increase investment and increase upstream investment.

Planting will increase, and recycling chains will become more complete. Therefore, in the future, we will see more and more things like the aluminum cans we use today. **GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Therefore, in the next five to ten years, could it be understood that materials such as biological materials may have greater potential to replace traditional plastic packaging?

**Mr. Cheng**

Of course, there is another perspective. From my understanding, biological materials indeed greatly reduce our dependence on petroleum. Because the source of traditional plastics largely comes from petroleum, while biological materials can be recycled continuously.

Reducing dependence on petroleum means environmental protection, because our extraction of resources from nature will decrease. In addition, when bio-based materials have higher costs, people will use them more carefully and be more willing to choose fewer disposable materials or materials that cannot be reused. This reflects a certain mindset, which is different from commercial decisions.

Of course, if the scale of cultivation and other factors reach a certain level, one day in the future the cost of bio-based materials may become lower than petroleum-based materials, which would be more ideal for society and the environment. However, what I want to say is that some companies are currently using bio-based materials, but there will be some intermediate steps. Because it is difficult for everyone to suddenly transition from traditional plastics to very environmentally friendly solutions. Cost, regulations, the transformation cycle of companies, and consumer acceptance are all factors that need to be considered.

For example, M&M’s packaging originally used multi-layer plastic packaging materials, but now they hope to move toward single-material development. However, it can be seen that this is relatively difficult. What they are doing is switching to other materials, such as using PLA or PE combined with paper packaging to replace ester-based packaging to a certain extent.

Actually, paper packaging machines are also being widely used. What you mentioned — is that bio-based?

Paper is another type of bio-based material. Currently, there are products moving in this direction: a single-layer film combined with ester-based materials. First, this reduces the use of plastic. In addition, when it is decomposed in the future, it will be easier, and there will be more opportunities for direct decomposition and reuse.

The entire industrial chain needed to be transferred, and existing production environments and processes also needed to be changed.

Using new products is one aspect, but existing production processes and environments also need to change.

Consumers at the end of the chain need a long time to accept these changes. Therefore, this is not very ideal. Everyone has certain steps, with a clear first step, second step, and some intermediate states.

This is quite a complicated process.
