Given the intense exercise, frequent sweating, and concentration of people in gyms, we think body odor may affect the gym environment and members' overall experience. We would like to understand your observations, current management practices, and potential uses for underarm deodorants in gyms.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Since members often sweat heavily during exercise, is body odor or underarm odor a recurring concern in daily gym operations and environmental management?

**Anonymous**

Yes. Some members have noticeable underarm odor, although they are a very small minority. We usually start by reminding them to manage it. If they do not, we may buy sprays or other deodorizing products for them.

This also applies to foot odor or other body odors. If the smell affects other people, we provide something they can use to address it themselves.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How much does this affect members' overall experience?

**Anonymous**

The person concerned may not feel that it affects them much because they are used to it. From our perspective as staff, however, it can have a substantial impact on other members' experience.

People have certain expectations of the environment when they come to a gym. They do not want unpleasant odors, dirt, or disorder. Members sometimes complain that another person has strong body odor, and we address those complaints promptly.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Do you think expectations concerning body odor and the overall environment differ between gyms with different market positions, such as standard and premium gyms?

**Anonymous**

Yes. Larger chain gyms, such as those occupying more than 2,000 or 3,000 square meters, may have more specific measures, including advising members to address the odor or recommending particular products.

Smaller gyms may simply remind a member that their body odor is noticeable and suggest ways to manage it, without having more targeted measures in place.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Have you encountered situations where a member's or trainer's body odor affected other members?

**Anonymous**

Yes. It is less common among trainers because they tend to pay attention to their professional image.

Some trainers may develop body odor or underarm odor after training, and we address that promptly. Sprays and topical medications are commonly used.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How would the gym usually respond to a complaint about a member's strong body odor?

**Anonymous**

We first improve ventilation in the changing rooms, including turning on the air conditioning. We then remind the member to address the issue personally.

If the member repeatedly ignores these reminders, we may cancel and refund their membership in more serious cases.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Can discussing body odor directly with a member be sensitive or embarrassing?

**Anonymous**

It can be awkward, but we do not immediately ask someone to leave. We give repeated reminders and suggest strategies or products.

We may also pay for products ourselves to help them manage it. However, if the odor persists and other members continue to complain, we may have to refund the membership. We cannot keep receiving complaints every day because of one member.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Does the gym mainly rely on members to manage their own body odor?

**Anonymous**

Yes, self-management is the main approach. We encourage members to maintain good personal hygiene. We only intervene and help when the odor is strong enough to affect other members. Otherwise, we generally do not get involved.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

From your observations, is it common for members to use underarm deodorants, antiperspirants, or similar personal care products?

**Anonymous**

Not particularly. Some members sweat heavily and have noticeable odor after exercise, but few bring these products with them.

When we have spoken to them, some have said that they manage the issue after showering, but the odor becomes strong again after training.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Do you think carrying or using these products would be convenient for people who exercise regularly?

**Anonymous**

If there were a product that noticeably reduced odor after someone had been sweating during training, it would be very useful for gyms.

Someone cannot be expected to stop halfway through a workout or a run just because they have started sweating. A product that could help in that situation would be ideal.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

If the gym provided underarm deodorant for members to help themselves to, like hand soap, tissues, or other personal care supplies, do you think there would be demand?

**Anonymous**

Only among a small proportion of members. Products such as hand soap are used much more widely.

If you put out an underarm deodorant, perhaps one in ten people, or two in fifty, would use it. It would be a very small group.

Some gyms, including larger brands, do not currently have a dedicated underarm deodorant dispenser or supply available for members to use, although they may keep products at the front desk.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

If a gym did provide such a product, what format would be most suitable?

**Anonymous**

A spray or a topical product, preferably a spray. It is convenient, can address the odor quickly, and is relatively hygienic.

Something that requires washing or a complicated application process would be awkward to use.
