**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How would you evaluate the feasibility and expected deodorizing effects of our three proposed strategies?

**Ms. Zhu**

The precursor-binding strategy prevents bacteria from accessing the precursor. Its expected deodorizing effect is moderate because binding is stoichiometric: one binding molecule can capture only a limited number of precursor molecules, requiring a relatively high application concentration. The precursors are small molecules, making it difficult to design binding proteins with high affinity and specificity.

The engineered-enzyme strategy disrupts the precursor's structure. Its expected deodorizing effect is extremely high because catalytic amplification allows very small amounts of enzyme to have a substantial effect. However, stability is extremely low: these large proteins can readily become inactivated in complex cosmetic matrices and the challenging environment on the skin.

The transporter-blocking strategy competitively inhibits bacterial uptake. Its expected deodorizing effect is high because it blocks substrates from entering bacterial cells. The challenges include targeting complex transmembrane proteins, maintaining the blocker's specific three-dimensional structure, and the potential for bacterial adaptive mutations.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How accepted is synthetic biology for producing cosmetic actives, and what challenges might AI-designed peptides face in skincare applications?

**Ms. Zhu**

Synthetic biology is very widely accepted in cosmetics, with examples such as recombinant collagen and ergothioneine. It is a rapidly growing field.

However, AI-designed peptides face a gap between computational predictions and biological reality. Their predicted structures and affinities may perform well under idealized conditions in silico, but real sweat contains salts and lipids and varies in pH. These conditions can alter peptide folding and cause binding to fail.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What technical and safety risks could arise from using competitive inhibitory proteins against bacterial precursor transporters on the skin? Could this disrupt the microbiome? In particular, how might reducing Staphylococcus uptake of Cys-Gly-3M3SH affect its survival and metabolism?

**Ms. Zhu**

Blocking uptake generally does not directly kill bacteria, so it is less likely to cause the severe disruption of the skin microbiome associated with broad-spectrum antibiotics.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Could long-term use of proteins or peptides that block precursor uptake lead to resistance through transporter mutations or changes in metabolic pathways? How could this adaptation be detected or prevented?

**Ms. Zhu**

Of the three strategies, long-term use of the transporter-blocking approach is the most likely to lead to resistance. Bacteria could evade the blockade by increasing the expression of alternative transporters or mutating the targeted transporter's binding site.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Could our design unintentionally affect beneficial skin bacteria, such as Staphylococcus epidermidis? How should this be evaluated?

**Ms. Zhu**

You could use serial passaging in a three-dimensional skin model containing a mixed microbial community, combined with 16S rRNA sequencing or metagenomic sequencing. This would allow you to monitor changes in microbial diversity and mutations in the target bacteria over time, and assess unintended effects on beneficial bacteria such as Staphylococcus epidermidis.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How stable are peptides in common deodorant formulations, including aqueous systems, alcohol-based systems, emulsions, and solid sticks?

**Ms. Zhu**

In aqueous systems and emulsions, prolonged exposure to water can cause hydrolysis, especially at extreme pH.

High concentrations of ethanol, often used in conventional antiperspirant sprays, can denature and inactivate most proteins with complex secondary or tertiary structures.

Solid sticks are the best option. Anhydrous systems, such as wax-based or silicone-based formulations, minimize hydrolysis and degradation.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How are peptides usually protected in topical products?

**Ms. Zhu**

Liposomes, solid lipid nanoparticles, or microencapsulation are commonly used to isolate peptides from moisture and the external environment.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Our peptides need to contact specific molecules at sweat gland openings or on the skin surface. What are the most common causes of inactivation under real conditions involving sweat, sebum, pH changes, and enzymes?

**Ms. Zhu**

The most likely causes in the underarm environment are enzymatic degradation and pH fluctuations. Proteases produced by the skin and resident bacteria can rapidly cleave peptides. Sweat pH generally fluctuates between 4.0 and 6.0, potentially causing precipitation at the protein's isoelectric point or unfolding.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Which product format offers the greatest advantages for maintaining activity and retention on the skin: a roll-on, spray, or cream?

**Ms. Zhu**

I would rank a solid balm stick first, followed by a roll-on and then a spray.

You should avoid making novel bioactive proteins into aerosols or sprays because of the significant risk of inhalation into the lungs, which I consider toxicologically unacceptable.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Are there known incompatibilities between peptides and preservatives or other common skincare ingredients?

**Ms. Zhu**

Peptides can be incompatible with strong preservatives, such as phenoxyethanol or certain organic acids, which may disrupt protein structure.

Trace metal ions in the formulation can cause peptide precipitation, requiring chelators such as EDTA. Anionic surfactants can disrupt the protein's charge balance and cause denaturation.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Which delivery systems help peptides remain active on the skin, particularly in the underarm environment?

**Ms. Zhu**

Cationic polymer encapsulation or cationic liposomes are often used to help peptides remain on the underarms despite sweat. Skin and hair carry a slight negative charge, so cationic systems can adhere electrostatically and create a prolonged micro-patch effect.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How can aggregation and degradation be prevented in the formulation? What stabilization strategies are commonly used?

**Ms. Zhu**

Stabilizers are essential. Polyols such as glycerol and trehalose help maintain the protein's hydration shell. Amino acids such as arginine and glycine help prevent nonspecific aggregation. A carefully controlled buffer system is also needed to maintain pH.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What toxicity or skin irritation tests would peptide-based deodorant actives require?

**Ms. Zhu**

In addition to routine in vitro cytotoxicity testing, peptides require Human Repeat Insult Patch Testing to assess long-term skin irritation and sensitization, as well as phototoxicity and photoallergy testing to check for harmful effects under ultraviolet exposure.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

What concentration ranges are generally considered safe for experimental bioactive ingredients?

**Ms. Zhu**

Bioactive peptides are typically effective and safe at very low concentrations, at the parts-per-million level, which is an advantage. However, entirely new AI-designed proteins are foreign antigens and present a very high risk of immune or allergic reactions.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Would a peptide deodorant that changes bacterial uptake pathways be classified as a cosmetic or a drug?

**Ms. Zhu**

That depends on the claims and mechanism. If you claim that it kills bacteria or changes physiological sweating, it would be classified as a drug or disinfectant product.

If its mechanism is to adsorb odor molecules or mask or prevent body odor without affecting normal skin physiology, and it does not have broad-spectrum bactericidal activity, it could be submitted as a cosmetic.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

Which authorities would regulate the product in China? Would a novel AI-designed bioactive protein face additional approval challenges, and what safety data would be needed?

**Ms. Zhu**

In China, it would fall under the National Medical Products Administration.

The greatest challenge is that an ingredient not listed in the Inventory of Existing Cosmetic Ingredients in China would require new cosmetic ingredient registration or notification. Biotechnology-derived new ingredients undergo very strict review. Their specific biological activity may require more extensive toxicology data, potentially including systemic toxicity, teratogenicity, and mutagenicity data. The approval process can be lengthy.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How can peptide retention at sweat gland openings or on the skin be measured or improved? Are there standardized wash-resistance or sweat-resistance tests?

**Ms. Zhu**

There is currently no standardized wash-resistance test specifically for deodorant peptides. The approach is usually adapted from sunscreen testing, drawing on the water-resistance testing logic of ISO 24444. After application, sweating or rinsing can be simulated, followed by tape stripping of the stratum corneum and LC-MS quantification of residual peptide.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

How are cosmetic peptides produced, and what are the main cost barriers for consumer skincare products?

**Ms. Zhu**

Solid-phase peptide synthesis is suitable for short peptides, usually fewer than 30 amino acids. It provides high purity, but costs increase exponentially with length.

Recombinant expression and fermentation are suitable for longer peptides or proteins. Production costs can be low at scale, but downstream purification, including endotoxin and host-cell protein removal, is very difficult and expensive. This is also a key area of scrutiny in submissions to the NMPA.

**GreatBay-SCIE 2026**

For commercialization, which format would best balance cost and activity: freeze-dried powder, ampoules, or a ready-to-use leave-on product?

**Ms. Zhu**

If you cannot maintain stability for more than three months in an aqueous system or emulsion, a premium option would be single-use or dual-chamber packaging. One compartment would contain freeze-dried protein powder and the other the formulation base, mixed by the consumer before first use. However, this would only suit a high-end product line.

For the mass market, an anhydrous antiperspirant balm or solid stick offers the best balance of cost, stability, and consumer habits.
