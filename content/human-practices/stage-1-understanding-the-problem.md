## Cycle 1 Inspiration and Public Survey

### Observe

When one of our [team members](/description#inspiration) was sharing her personal troubles with underarm odor, she also brought up **negative reviews** she had encountered regarding antiperspirant and deodorant products. This led us to question whether others faced similar problems. We therefore examined numerous products and their user reviews, soon noticing recurring concerns such as **itching, redness** and **burning sensations on skin.**

We recognized that current products have notable limitations, this prompted us to consider whether we could explore a synthetic biology-based solution. To further validate these observations and understand users' needs, we decided on taking action to collect broader **stakeholder feedback**.

### Determine

We gathered stakeholder perspectives via an **online survey** shared with local Chinese communities, receiving a total of **486 responses**. The survey focused on investigating sweating conditions, deodorant usage habits, adverse reactions to current products, preferences for a desired deodorant and attitudes towards a synthetic biology based solution.

### Outcome

Based on our survey results, **21.44%** of responses reported using deodorant, given China's large population, this percentage represents a substantial number of consumers, making it a significant market.

Interestingly, **40.19%** of results were unsatisfied with current products, and **11.21%** of respondents reported physical discomfort, specifically **rashes**, **irritation** and **stuffy sensations** in the underarm area.

Finally, we observed that **73.55%** of responses showed a positive attitude towards trying a synthetic biology based solution, indicating high market potential for our product.

### Refine

Given **40.19%** of results were unsatisfied with current products, this indicated room for **improvement** in existing products. As a result, we identified [**regular deodorant users**](/human-practices/stage-2-identifying-unmet-needs#cycle-1-deodorant-users) as potential customers and sought to further understand their needs, preferences, and expectations for an improved product.

As we identified **physical discomfort** as a common concern among respondents, we were prompted to investigate the underlying mechanism responsible for these adverse reactions.

To meet the preferences of desired fragrance, we saw that the vast majority of respondents preferred unscented products. In response, we decided to offer both scented and unscented options, focusing on woody and floral fragrances for those who prefer scented products.

Finally, we noticed that there were still **26.45%** of responses expressing unwillingness towards using synthetic biology products. We accredit this to a **lack of understanding** of this field, particularly its mechanisms, applications and potential value in products. This motivated us to carry out many [**educational initiatives**](/education) in hopes of raising awareness of the general public about this matter.

## Cycle 2 Dermatologists

### Observe

As **physical discomfort** was a joint concern identified through our survey, we wanted to gain a better understanding of its **possible causes** and the general **safety** of current deodorant products.

### Determine

To gain a **clinical perspective** on existing approaches to deal with underarm odor and the concerns commonly faced by patients, we interviewed **Dr. Xia Yihui**, a dermatologist and member of several professional dermatology and medical aesthetics committees in China, including the Chinese Medical Doctor Association's Dermatologist Branch.

### Outcome

Dr. Xia started by explaining that underarm odor was caused by the metabolism of naturally occurring skin bacteria *Staphylococcus hominis* and that **genetic factors** are the main determinant of its occurrence. He shared the research paper ***Structural Basis of Malodour Precursor Transport in the Human Axilla (Minhas et al., 2018)*** with us, which served as the basis for our subsequent research. :fn-ref{id="1"}

Dr. Xia then introduced several current conventional approaches to managing underarm odor, including topical treatments, antibacterial products, and antiperspirants, most of which have evident limitations. For example, **topical (direct application to skin) treatments**, because of direct contact with skin, have higher risks of adverse skin reactions  such as redness, itching, dryness, pigmentation, or contact dermatitis. On the other hand, **antibacterial products** may disrupt the skin's natural microbial balance. Dr. Xia explained that the skin hosts a diverse community of microorganisms, and that broad-spectrum antibacterial products can affect both beneficial and harmful bacteria. With prolonged use, these changes may compromise the skin barrier and increase the risk of infections or other skin problems. Likewise, **antiperspirants** block sweat secretion altogether, which if sustained could cause skin irritation due to regional overheating as sweat is unable to cool the temperature down.

Dr. Xia also mentioned **Surgery** and **Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM)** as alternative treatment options for underarm odor, which we had not previously considered. As he does not specialize in either field, he recommended that we consult specialists in these areas for more detailed information.

Beyond offering clinical guidance, Dr. Xia also mentioned that, if our product proved effective and safe for patients, the hospital would be happy to explore a **collaboration** to make it available for patients to purchase.

### Refine

Based on Dr. Xia's insights, we investigated the research paper he provided and conducted a broader literature review to understand the underlying mechanisms of underarm odor. This ultimately laid the foundation for our [novel mechanism-based approach](/model).

His interest also helped us identify **hospitals as potential collaborators** and prompted us to explore opportunities for partnerships with [other institutions](/human-practices/stage-2-identifying-unmet-needs#cycle-3-gym-and-hotel-owners).

At the same time, we decided to explore **Surgical Treatment** and **Traditional Chinese Medicine** as alternative solutions, seeking potential ingredients for our formulation.

## Cycle 3 Axillary Surgeon

### Observe

After Dr. Xia identified **surgery** as a treatment option for underarm odor, we became interested in understanding how surgical interventions fit into the broader landscape of existing treatments.

### Determine

We therefore interviewed **Dr. Yang Jianhong**, Associate Chief Surgeon at Yuelu District People's Hospital in Changsha, who has extensive experience performing various surgical procedures for underarm odor. We aimed to gain insights from his clinical experience, specifically its advantages, disadvantages and potential areas for improvement.

### Outcome

Dr. Yang explained that there are three main surgical approaches treating underarm odor: minimally invasive sweat gland removal, radiofrequency ablation, and the traditional excision surgery. He said that all three approaches can effectively reduce underarm odor. **Minimally invasive surgery** removes the apocrine glands through a small incision and can provide long-lasting or potentially permanent results. **Radiofrequency treatment** uses heat energy to target the apocrine glands and is less invasive, with a recovery period of approximately one to two weeks. **Traditional excision surgery** removes part of the skin and the underlying apocrine glands, making it more invasive but a suitable option for patients with more severe or recurrent cases.

Overall, Dr. Yang spoke **positively** about surgical treatment, emphasizing that it provides better results than non-surgical approaches.

Although Dr. Yang spoke highly of surgical treatments, he also acknowledged that it is not without risks. According to his estimates, **scarring** occurs in approximately **10–15%** of cases, **hematoma** in **5–10%**, **infection** in around **3%**, and **skin necrosis** in around **1%**. Patients may also experience **temporary sensory abnormalities** caused by damage to subcutaneous nerve endings. In addition, surgery requires a relatively long recovery period, and its effects are **not always permanent**, as underarm odor may recur after treatment.

Because of these risks and the physical demands of surgery, he noted that it is **not suitable for everyone**. In particular, he advised against surgical treatment for **teenagers**, whose apocrine glands are still maturing during puberty, making the extent of odor and the long-term outcomes of surgery less predictable. For **pregnant patients**, he recommended postponing surgery until after childbirth, as the procedure is generally non-urgent and this would avoid unnecessary concerns regarding anesthesia, medication, and fetal health.

### Refine

We realized that the **safety risks** and **long recovery period** of surgery made it unsuitable for certain groups. This led us to **prioritize safety** as one of our key design priorities. We also expanded our potential customer base to people who could not or preferred not to undergo surgery, such as adolescents and the pregnant, and to focus on addressing their unmet needs.

Additionally, we acknowledged that **recurrence** remained a concern even after surgical treatment, leading us to later investigate this issue from a [patient's perspective](/human-practices/stage-2-identifying-unmet-needs#cycle-2-people-having-undergone-axillary-surgery).

## Cycle 4 Traditional Chinese Medicine Specialist

### Observe

Dr. Yang's mention of **Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM)** introduced us to another potential approach to managing underarm odor.

### Determine

We therefore interviewed **Dr. Hu Zhenhua**, a TCM doctor experienced in treating underarm odor-related conditions, to understand the mechanisms behind TCM treatments and explore whether any ingredients could be incorporated into Expelliodor.

### Outcome

Dr Hu explained that TCM saw underarm odor as a result of internal imbalance, particularly **"liver fire" (肝火)** and **"damp-heat accumulation" (湿热蕴积)**, which may arise from stagnation of **"liver Qi" (肝气)**. Based on this understanding, two treatment approaches were proposed.

**Internal treatment** such as "**Longdan Xiegan" (龙胆泻肝)** focused on clearing heat and regulating metabolism. However, such substances may cause adverse effects, including nausea, headaches, and rashes, without directly addressing the underlying cause of underarm odor.

**External treatment** focused on damaging parts of the sweat glands. Especially ingredients like "**Qingfen" (轻粉)**, more commonly known as mercurous chloride are extremely dangerous and are now banned in modern medicine. As these ingredients proved to be harmful, TCM is used **less frequently** for underarm odor, with many TCM doctors directing their patients towards surgical or dermatological approaches instead.

Furthermore, the doctor stated that TCM treatments are not particularly effective and often require repeated treatment, which is also why these approaches are not commonly used today. This creates a compromise to be made between **effectiveness** and **safety**, as insufficient amounts may not achieve the desired effect, while excessive amounts can raise safety concerns.

### Refine

Our interview showed that TCM could not provide a suitable ingredient for our product. More broadly, it reinforced a pattern we had seen across our research, existing treatments often involve trade-offs between factors such as **effectiveness** and **safety**. This led us to consider whether a [**novel ingredient**](/human-practices/stage-3-developing-solutions#cycle-1-modeling-expert) could help address these limitations and provide a better balance.

## Moving On

By the end of **Stage 1**, we had identified several initial **potential customer groups**:

- **People dissatisfied with existing deodorants**, particularly those experiencing skin discomfort;

- **People who could not or preferred not to undergo surgery**, including adolescents and those with pregnancy-related safety concerns;

- **People experiencing odor recurrence after surgery**；

- and **anyone who wishes to manage their underarm odor**.

These findings guided our subsequent interviews with potential customers, through which we sought to understand their experiences and unmet needs firsthand.

We also identified **hospitals as potential collaborators**, subject to further validation of our product's effectiveness and safety, and began exploring **potential partnerships** with other institutions.
