## Cycle 1 Inspiration and Public Survey

### Observe

When one of our team members was sharing her personal experiences with underarm odor, she also brought up **negative reviews** she had encountered regarding antiperspirant and deodorant products. This led us to question whether others faced similar problems. We therefore examined numerous products and their user reviews, soon noticing recurring concerns such as **skin irritation, residue on clothing, and skin discoloration**.

To further validate these observations and understand users' needs, we decided on taking action to collect broader **stakeholder feedback**.

### Determine

We gathered stakeholder perspectives via an **online survey** shared with local Chinese communities, receiving a total of **486 responses**. The survey focused on investigating sweating conditions, deodorant usage habits, adverse reactions to current products, preferences for a desired deodorant and attitudes towards a synthetic biology based solution.

Based on our survey results, **21.44%** of responses reported using deodorant, given China's large population, this percentage represents a substantial number of consumers, making it a significant market.

Interestingly, **40.19%** of results were unsatisfied with current products, and **11.21 %** of respondents reported physical discomfort, specifically rashes, irritation and stuffy sensations in the underarm area. This indicates room for improvement in existing products.

Additionally, floral and woody scents were the most popular choices after unscented products.

Finally, we observed that **73.55%** of responses showed a positive attitude towards trying a synthetic biology based solution, indicating high market potential for our product.

### Refine

As we identified **physical discomfort** as a common concern among respondents, we were prompted to investigate the underlying mechanism responsible for these adverse reactions.

To meet the preferences of desired fragrance, we carried out literature research and identified [**geranyl butyrate** (responsible for fruity and floral scents) and **bornyl acetate** (responsible for woody scents)](/engineering/terpenoid-ester) as suitable candidates for our source of scent.

Finally, we noticed **26.45%** of responses expressing unwillingness towards using synthetic biology products. We accredit this to a lack of understanding of this field, particularly its mechanisms, applications and potential value in products. This motivated us to carry out many [**educational initiatives**](/education) in hopes of educating the general public about this matter.

## Cycle 2 Dermatologist

### Observe

As **physical discomfort** was a joint concern identified through our survey, we wanted to gain a better understanding of its **possible causes** and the general **safety** of current deodorant products.

### Determine

To achieve this, we interviewed **Dr. Xia Yonghui**, a dermatologist and member of several professional dermatology and medical aesthetics committees in China, including the Chinese Medical Doctor Association's Dermatologist Branch, to gain a clinical perspective on existing approaches to deal with underarm odor and the concerns commonly faced by patients.

### Outcome

During the interview, Dr. Xia highlighted the importance of **understanding the cause** of underarm odor rather than focusing solely on its treatment. He explained that the odor was caused by the metabolism of naturally occurring skin bacteria and that **genetic factors** are the main determinant of its occurrence. He shared the research paper***Structural Basis of Malodour Precursor Transport in the Human Axilla (Minhas et al., 2018)** *with us, which served as the basis for our subsequent research. :fn-ref{id="1"}

Dr. Xia then introduced several current conventional approaches to managing axillary odor, including topical treatments, antibacterial products, and antiperspirants, most of which have evident limitations. For example, **topical(direct application to skin) treatments**, because of direct contact with skin, have higher risks of adverse skin reactionssuch as redness, itching, dryness, pigmentation, or contact dermatitis. On the other hand, **antibacterial products** may disrupt the skin's natural microbial balance. Dr. Xia explained that the skin hosts a diverse community of microorganisms, and that broad-spectrum antibacterial products can affect both beneficial and harmful bacteria. With prolonged use, these changes may compromise the skin barrier and increase the risk of infections or other skin problems. Likewise, **antiperspirants** block sweat secretion altogether, which if sustained could cause skin irritation due to regional overheating as sweat is unable to cool the temperature down.

Dr. Xia also mentioned **Surgery** and **Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM)** as alternative treatment options for underarm odor, which we had not previously considered. As he does not specialize in either field, he recommended that we consult specialists in these areas for more detailed information.

### Refine

Based on Dr. Xia's insights, we investigated the research paper he provided and conducted a broader literature review to understand the underlying mechanisms of underarm odor.

At the same time, we decided to explore **Surgical Treatment** and **Traditional Chinese Medicine** as alternative solutions, seeking potential ingredients for our formulation.

## Cycle 3 Axillary Surgeon

### Observe

After Dr. Xia identified **surgery** as a treatment option for underarm odor, we wanted to explore this approach in greater depth, specifically its advantages, disadvantages and potential areas for improvement.

### Determine

We therefore interviewed **Dr. Yang Jianhong**, Associate Chief Surgeon at Yuelu District People's Hospital in Changsha, who has extensive experience performing a wide range of procedures for underarm odor.

### Outcome

Dr. Yang explained that there are three main surgical approaches treating underarm odor: minimally invasive sweat gland removal, radiofrequency ablation, and the traditional excision surgery. He said that all three approaches can effectively reduce underarm odor. **Minimally invasive surgery** removes the apocrine glands through a small incision and can provide long-lasting or potentially permanent results. **Radiofrequency treatment** uses heat energy to target the apocrine glands and is less invasive, with a recovery period of approximately one to two weeks. **Traditional excision surgery** removes part of the skin and the underlying apocrine glands, making it more invasive but a suitable option for patients with more severe or recurrent cases.

Overall, Dr. Yang spoke **positively** about surgical treatment, emphasizing that it provides better results than non-surgical approaches. He stated that surgical treatments have a patient satisfaction rate of around **90%** and explained that they are a routine part of his department's work, with around **200–300 cases** performed each year. He personally performs approximately **100–150 cases** annually.

Although Dr. Yang spoke highly of surgical treatments, he also acknowledged that it is not without risks. According to his estimates, **scarring** occurs in approximately **10–15%** of cases, **hematoma** in **5–10%**, **infection** in around **3%**, and **skin necrosis** in around **1%**. Patients may also experience **temporary sensory abnormalities** caused by damage to subcutaneous nerve endings. In addition, surgery requires a relatively long recovery period, and its effects are **not always permanent**, as underarm odor may recur after treatment.

Because of these risks and the physical demands of surgery, he noted that it is **not suitable for everyone**. In particular, he advised against surgical treatment for **teenagers**, whose apocrine sweat glands are still maturing during puberty, making the extent of odor and the long-term outcomes of surgery less predictable. For **pregnant patients**, he recommended postponing surgery until after childbirth, as the procedure is generally non-urgent and this would avoid unnecessary concerns regarding anesthesia, medication, and fetal health.

### Refine

We realized that the safety risks and long recovery period of surgery made it unsuitable for certain groups. This led us to prioritize **safety** as one of our key design priorities. We also expanded our customer base to people who could not or preferred not to undergo surgery and to focus on addressing their unmet needs.

Additionally, we acknowledged that **recurrence** remained a concern even after surgical treatment, leading us to later investigate this issue from a patient's perspective.

## Cycle 4 Traditional Chinese Medicine Specialist

### Observe

Building on Dr. Yang's mention of **Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM)** as an alternative approach to managing underarm odor, we were curious about its mechanism and whether it contained any suitable ingredients for our product.

### Determine

To better understand the context of current solutions and to address a broader range of treatments, we interviewed **Dr. Hu Zhenhua**, a TCM doctor experienced in treating body odor-related conditions.

### Outcome

Dr Hu explained that TCM saw underarm odor as a result of internal imbalance, particularly **"liver fire" (肝火)** and **"damp-heat accumulation" (湿热蕴积)**, which may arise from stagnation of liver **"Qi" (气)**. Based on this understanding, two treatment approaches were proposed.

**Internal treatment** such as **Longdan Xiegan (龙胆泻肝)** focused on clearing heat and regulating metabolism. However, substances like this may cause nausea, headaches, rashes and other adverse effects. **External treatment** focused on damaging parts of the sweat glands and may be toxic. Especially ingredients like **Qingfen(轻粉)**, more commonly known as mercurous chloride are extremely dangerous and are now banned in modern medicine. As these ingredients proved to be harmful, TCM is used less frequently for underarm odor, with many TCM doctors directing their patients towards surgical or dermatological approaches instead. Furthermore, the doctor stated that TCM treatments are not particularly effective and often require repeated treatment, which is also why these approaches are not commonly used today.

This creates a compromise to be made between **effectiveness** and **safety**, as insufficient amounts may not achieve the desired effect, while excessive amounts can raise safety concerns.

### Refine

Our interview showed that TCM could not provide a suitable ingredient for our product. More broadly, it reinforced a pattern we had seen across our research, existing treatments often involve trade-offs between factors such as **effectiveness** and **safety**. This led us to consider whether a **novel ingredient** could help address these limitations and provide a better balance.
