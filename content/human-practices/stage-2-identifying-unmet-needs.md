## Cycle 1 Deodorant Users

### Observe

From our dermatologist interview, we learned about existing products for underarm odor. To understand their **limitations** in everyday use and **what potential customers expected** from an ideal product, we needed to hear directly from deodorant users.

### Determine

We interviewed users, **anonymous teammate**, **Mr. Chen** and **Mr. Wu,** of different ages and genders, with varying levels of self-reported underarm odor and experience using different deodorants. This diversity allowed us to gain insights into the different perspectives, priorities, and concerns surrounding underarm odor management.

### Outcome

The first interviewee, our teammate who described her underarm odor as moderate, had tried several deodorants without finding their effects satisfactory. She used deodorant daily, saying, **"without it, I feel really uneasy, constantly worrying that others will notice the odor and distance themselves from me or leave me out."**

**Mr. Chen**, a **man in his sixties** with self-reported mild underarm odor, recalled experiencing **sore underarms and an allergic reaction** after using a deodorant. His symptoms became severe enough to require hospital treatment, where he received medication to manage the allergic reaction to relieve the underarm inflammation.

**Mr. Wu**, a **man in his fifties** with moderate underarm odor, was relatively satisfied with the effectiveness of his methenamine deodorant. However, he disliked the **dryness** and **peeling** he experienced, describing his skin as resembling **"toad skin"**.

Across the interviews, users expected a single application to provide protection for 3 days to 1 week. Adult interviewees generally prioritized **effectiveness** and considered prices **below RMB 100** acceptable, whereas our teenage interviewee preferred a more affordable price of **RMB 20–30**. Preferences for application formats varied, with roll-ons, sprays, and ointments all being popular choices.

All three interviewees began using deodorants around **middle or high school**, when puberty made underarm odor more noticeable. Increased social interactions and greater concern about others' opinions during adolescence also increased their concern about underarm odor.

### Refine

These conversations helped us identify several unmet needs: **long lasting** odor control, **gentle** ingredients that are **safe for underarm skin**, a **low risk of irritation** or allergic reactions, and **affordability**.

Our findings also highlighted adolescents as a primary target group, as heightened social awareness due to puberty often prompts them to seek odor control. To address their needs, we planned to design a dedicated product line prioritizing **affordability and gentle yet effective odor control during puberty.**

## Cycle 2  People having undergone Axillary Surgery

### Observe

Our interview with an axillary surgeon introduced **surgery** as a potential long-term solution for underarm odor, prompting us to explore its outcomes from a patient's perspective.

### Determine

We therefore reached out to **Ms. Chen**, who had undergone surgery several years before. We wanted to explore the **long-term outcomes of surgery**, including how effective it was over time and whether they experienced any return of odor.

### Outcome

Ms. Chen described her underarm odor before surgery as moderate. Concerned about using conventional deodorants during a future pregnancy, particularly after her doctor advised avoiding them as a precaution, she opted for a **minimally invasive procedure** costing **over RMB 10,000**, hoping for a permanent solution. However, **her underarm odor eventually returned**, requiring her to continue using deodorant. She also found the **recovery period disruptive**, as movement restrictions interfered with her daily activities and forced her to take time off work.

Ms. Chen then shared the experience of a friend who had undergone a **more invasive procedure** to remove sweat glands. According to her account, the surgery had not completely eliminated the odor and had **left noticeable scars.**

### Refine

Ms. Chen's experience showed that surgery may not eliminate the need for ongoing odor management. The high cost, lengthy recovery and risk of scarring also helped explain why some users might prefer a **non-surgical option**, while her concerns about pregnancy further emphasized the importance of **product safety**.

## Cycle 3 Gym and Hotel Owners

### Observe

Beyond the hospital, we also considered **institutions such as gyms and hotels as potential collaborators**, who could retail our poducts in their place.

### Determine

To explore this possibility, we interviewed a **gym owner** and a **hotel owner** to understand how they viewed deodorant provision in their facilities and what requirements they would prioritize.

### Outcome

The gym owner recalled an incident in which a gym member complained about another member's underarm odor. In such situations, gym staff would initially try to hint at the issue indirectly, but if this did not work, they would offer deodorant products to help address the situation.

However, the owner felt that selling deodorants directly at the gym might not be practical, as most members already manage their body odor independently, potentially limiting demand. Instead, he **suggested that gyms purchase deodorants in bulk** and make them available in changing rooms for members to use when needed.

Regarding product design, he preferred **spray deodorants** because they would be easier for different gym users to share. He also emphasized the importance of **rapid**, **affordable**, and **long-lasting** odor control.

Similarly, the hotel owner did not consider selling deodorants directly to guests. Instead, he suggested **including them in hotel toiletry kits or making them available at the reception** for guests who needed them.

**Cost was his main consideration**, as hotels would need to manage expenses when purchasing products in larger quantities. He also preferred **single-use products**, which would be easier to replace and more hygienenic.

### Refine

This interview confirmed that, beyond **individual consumers**, supplying deodorants to institutions such as gyms and hotels could be a viable sales channel. Since these customers may purchase in larger quantities, we plan to offer **bulk-purchase discounts** as an additional service. This also encouraged us to explore other potential [**secondary customer groups**](/entrepreneurship#secondary-end-users-and-customers) for future expansion.

The gym owner's feedback also reinforced the importance of **convenience, affordability, and fast action** in shared facilities. We therefore **retained the spray format** as an option suited to gym environments.

Meanwhile, the hotel owner's preference for single-use products prompted us to consider alternative formats that could be replaced after each use. We therefore began exploring an alternative format: [**single-use stickers applied to clothing.**](/hardware)
