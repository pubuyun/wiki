Through Characterize–Design–Build–Test–Learn (C-DBTL) cycles, we developed and refined protein components of Expelliodor. Their functional effects were evaluated through whole-cell assays using a PepT<sub>Sh</sub>-expressing *E. coli* surrogate. Several of the tested precursor binders, transporter binders, and CGTase variants reduced thiol levels relative to their respective controls in whole-cell assays, supporting their potential to limit odor formation. Building on these findings, we explored computational approaches to optimize our protein designs: *LASErMPNN* guided precursor-binder sequence optimization, while *SPURS* and mutation-compatibility analysis informed CGTase redesign for thermostability. Furthermore, to complement our odor-prevention strategy, we synthesized bornyl acetate and geranyl butyrate through two-strain co-cultures and incorporated them into Expelliodor’s design as fragrance components.

Following these engineering cycles, future work will focus on confirming the proposed binding and precursor-modification mechanisms, further characterizing CGTase thermostability, and improving terpenoid production yields. We also plan to test our engineered components in *S. hominis* to evaluate their effectiveness beyond the *E. coli* surrogate system, before exploring their integration into a final formulation and conducting safety assessments.
