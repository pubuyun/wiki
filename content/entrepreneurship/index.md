### Problem Statement

Axillary odor is a widespread biological and social concern that can negatively affect daily comfort, self-confidence, interpersonal communication, and social participation. Genetic variation in **ABCC11** strongly influences susceptibility to severe axillary odor: the odor-associated allele has been reported in approximately **99.9% of African populations, 85% of European populations, 70% of South Asian populations, and 5% of East Asian populations**. Even in East Asia, where the proportion is relatively low, 5% of its large population still represents **tens of millions of potentially affected individuals**. Limited public awareness may further intensify the **misunderstanding, stigma, and discrimination** experienced by people living with this condition.

Beyond physical discomfort, severe axillary odor may lead to **embarrassment**, **social exclusion**, and **anxiety**, making it more than a superficial cosmetic issue. However, many existing deodorants primarily mask odor, suppress perspiration using aluminum salts, or broadly inhibit skin bacteria. These approaches may provide only temporary control, irritate sensitive skin, or disturb the natural axillary microbiome without precisely addressing the biological process responsible for odor formation. Consequently, axillary odor remains a significant and insufficiently addressed personal-care challenge.

### Our Solutions

Expelliodor is a protein-based deodorant designed to tackle underarm odor before it forms. Underarm odor develops when *Staphylococcus hominis*, a bacterium naturally found on the skin, absorbs the odorless sweat precursor **Cys-Gly-3M3SH** and converts it into the strong-smelling compound **3M3SH**. Instead of broadly killing skin bacteria, Expelliodor targets this specific process through three complementary strategies:

- **Precursor Binder:** Captures the odor precursor on the skin surface, reducing the amount available for bacterial uptake.

- **Transporter Binder:** Blocks the bacterial transporter ***PepT<sub>Sh</sub>***, which is responsible for carrying the precursor into the bacterial cell.

- **CGTase:** Continuously modifies newly secreted precursor molecules, making them more difficult for the transporter to recognize and absorb.

Together, these strategies address both existing and newly secreted odor precursors, providing more targeted and sustained odor control while minimizing unnecessary disruption to the natural skin microbiome.

## Customer Discovery

### Customers Mapping

Based on our investigations and interviews with some potential customers during Stage 1 of our iHP, we have gradually clarified the definition and categorisation of our potential customers, as shown below:

#### Primary Customers

Expelliodor primarily targets the following three types of customers:

- **Individuals allergic to conventional deodorants**: sensitized to common allergens including fragrance blends (hydroxycitronellal) and aluminum‑zirconium complexes, unable to use standard deodorants safely

- **Individuals restricted from axillary osmidrosis surgery by clinical guidelines**: adolescents (incomplete gland development, high recurrence risk) and pregnant individuals (fetal safety concerns)

- **Users valuing underarm skin health and aesthetics**: wish to avoid dryness, flaking and pigmentation from traditional deodorants, as well as surgical scarring

Given that these users value direct product access and personalised consumer experience, we adopt both *B2B* (through offline retail) and *B2C* (through e-commerce) models to serve our primary customers.

#### Secondary End-Users and Customers

Expelliodor features a broad group of **secondary end‑users**, largely individuals **frequently exposed to socially demanding venues** such as:

- Beauty and personal care practitioners and clients

- Sports‑fitness venue staff and members

- Hospitality staff and guests

- Travel passengers and transport service crews

- Retail employees and shoppers

- Corporate office employees

These secondary end-users are not our direct customers. As they are spread across a wide range of public and commercial settings, we target the **organisations**that operate these venues as our **B2B customers**.

Rather than relying primarily on direct-to-consumer outreach, we adopt a B2B wholesale model, supplying products to partners such as airlines, hotels, and gyms at wholesale prices. These partners can then resell our products or make them available to their staff and customers, allowing us to reach end-users at scale.

We will work with our partners to create a seamless end-user experience, for example by placing our products at appropriate and accessible self-service points including hotel-lobby restrooms, gym front desks and other shared facilities.

#### Tertiary Customers

More broadly, Expelliodor's tertiary customers include anyone who is concerned about underarm odor.

### Unmet Needs Identification

Through extensive iHP outreach, including customer interviews, surveys and expert consultation, we identified the following unmet needs in existing deodorant products.

#### Reliable odor control

This need emerged from the teenage potential customer, who had tried several deodorants without satisfactory results, highlighting the need for more dependable effectiveness.

#### Lower irritation and allergy risks

This need emerged from Mr. Chen, who reported sores and an allergic reaction requiring medical treatment, and survey respondents who described physical discomfort.

#### Affordability, particularly for adolescents

This need emerged from the teenage interviewee, who preferred RMB 20–30, while adult interviewees generally accepted prices below RMB 100 if effectiveness was satisfactory.

#### Suitable fragrance options

This need emerged from Ms. Wu, who valued a pleasant scent, and local and international surveys showing different fragrance preferences.

#### Convenient formats and a comfortable skin feel

This need emerged from users expressing different preferences for roll-ons, sprays and creams, and Mr. Su’s advice to avoid complicated preparation and sticky formulations.

#### Minimal clothing stains and residue

This need emerged from international potential customer Mr. Islam and online product reviews examined early in the project, which highlighted concerns about clothing stains and residue.

#### Fast, hygienic odor management in shared facilities

This need emerged from the gym owner, who prioritized quick action and convenient shared use, and the hotel manager, who preferred affordable, replaceable single-use products.

#### Accessible packaging

This need emerged from inclusive-packaging research prompted by Ms. Troy’s comments: conventional caps and applicators may be difficult to use for older adults, people with limited hand mobility and people with visual impairments.

#### More sustainable packaging

This need emerged from Mr. Wood, who specifically valued environmentally friendly features such as refillable packaging.

Their perspectives shaped more respectful communication that emphasizes personal choice and guided our efforts to address misconceptions and support people experiencing concerns about axillary odor.

#### Total addressable market (TAM)

We define our TAM on a global scale, representing the entire potential consumer base for our product. The estimated TAM is **USD 51.75 billion**.

::content-components-collapsible-title{title="More Details" heading-tag="h5" id="tam-details"}
We estimated our TAM using the following formula: :fn-ref{#1}

$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{TAM}&=\text{Total Number of Potential Consumers}\\&\quad\times\text{Annual Revenue per Consumer}\end{aligned}$

To estimate the annual revenue per consumer, we calculated the annual consumer spending on deodorant products based on the average retail price and the average number of deodorant products purchased per year.

$\begin{aligned}\text{Annual Spending per Consumer}&=\text{Average Retail Price}\\&\quad\times\text{Number of Units Purchased Annually}\end{aligned}$

The estimated average price for a deodorant stick was **USD 4.50**. :fn-ref{#2} Based on consumer usage estimates, an individual purchases approximately **4–6 deodorant products per year**. :fn-ref{#3} Therefore:

$\text{Annual Spending per Consumer}=4.50\times\frac{4+6}{2}=\text{USD }22.50/\text{year}$

Based on our research, over 2.3 billion individuals worldwide were estimated to use deodorant regularly in 2024. :fn-ref{#4} As the deodorant market continues to grow, we use this latest available estimate as a conservative approximation of the global deodorant consumer base in 2026.

$\mathrm{TAM}=2.3\,\text{billion}\times22.50=\text{USD }51.75\,\text{billion}$

::

#### Serviceable Available Market (SAM)

We addressed our SAM based on the market conditions in Europe, the Asia Pacific and North America, with an estimation of **USD 33.64 billion** in total.

::content-components-collapsible-title{title="More Details" heading-tag="h5" id="sam-details"}
Market conditions are reviewed from the base year 2025.

:::content-components-lightbox-image

To evaluate our SAM, we selected Europe, North America, and the Asia Pacific as our target regions. These three regions dominate the global deodorant market, based on their global market share, *CAGR* and regulatory requirements. Europe represented the largest market among the three, with a 33.9% global market share in 2025. North America followed with a 27.1% share. Although the Asia-Pacific had a smaller market share of 25.1%, it had the highest projected CAGR of 6.76%, indicating stronger projected market growth. :fn-ref{#5}

Our formula for SAM is derived from the same source as our TAM calculation: :fn-ref{#1}

$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{SAM}&=\mathrm{TAM}\times\text{Target Segment Percentage}\\&\quad(\text{Global Market Share}\times\text{Open to SynBio})\end{aligned}$

$\text{Europe}=33.9\%$

$\text{Open to SynBio (from survey)}=80\%$

$\mathrm{SAM}=51.75\times33.9\%\times80\%=\text{USD }14.03\,\text{billion}$

$\text{North America}=27.1\%$

$\text{Open to SynBio (from survey)}=75\%$

$\mathrm{SAM}=51.75\times27.1\%\times75\%=\text{USD }10.52\,\text{billion}$

$\text{The Asia-Pacific}=25.1\%$

$\text{Open to SynBio (from survey)}=70\%$

$\mathrm{SAM}=51.75\times25.1\%\times70\%=\text{USD }9.09\,\text{billion}$

$\text{Total SAM}=14.03+10.52+9.09=\text{USD }33.64\,\text{billion}$

::

#### Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)

Based on the market conditions of different regions, we identified the expected obtainable market share of the Asia Pacific as our SOM, with an estimated **USD 0.27 billion**.

::content-components-collapsible-title{title="More Details" heading-tag="h5" id="som-details"}
Our methodology uses the equation: :fn-ref{#1}

$\mathrm{SOM}=\text{SAM of the Asia Pacific}\times\text{Expected Market Share Percentage}.$

As the expected market share for an early-stage startup typically ranges from 1% to 5%, we adopted the midpoint of this range, 3%, for our estimation. :fn-ref{#6}

$\mathrm{SOM}=9.09\times3\%=\text{USD }0.27\,\text{billion}$

::

#### Political

##### Opportunity 1 — APEC 2026 Shenzhen

APEC 2026, hosted by China with Shenzhen as a major meeting hub, emphasizes “Openness, Innovation and Cooperation”, with priorities including regional connectivity, digital economy, and support for SMEs (small and medium-sized enterprises). As Expelliodor is headquartered in Shenzhen, the company may benefit from the increased regional connectivity and business opportunities associated with APEC. China’s Deputy Foreign Minister Ma Zhaoxu expressed confidence in the further development of the *Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific* (FTAAP), as well as in strengthening cooperation and capacity-building among SMEs. :fn-ref{#10} APEC might lead to further reductions in tariffs and promote a multilateral trading system. This may create a more favorable environment for innovative consumer biotechnology products to expand across the Asia-Pacific markets. :fn-ref{#11}

##### Opportunity 2 — Reforms in NMPA's Regulations for Cosmetics

China’s *NMPA* has introduced reforms to facilitate the approval of cosmetics with new efficacy claims and to support technological innovation in new cosmetic ingredients, thereby reducing regulatory barriers for innovative formulations. :fn-ref{#12} This could allow our newly synthesized proteins, designed to precisely target 3M3SH for deodorant effects, to benefit from a more supportive regulatory environment, potentially lowering early-stage commercialization barriers and strengthening investor confidence in the product’s development prospects.

##### Challenge 1 — Fragmented Regulations across Regional Markets

Cosmetic regulatory frameworks vary considerably across countries, with differences in product classification, ingredient restrictions, safety assessment, labeling, and registration requirements. As a result, compliance achieved in one market cannot necessarily be transferred directly to another, increasing the time required, cost, and complexity of international expansion. :fn-ref{#13}

#### Economic

##### Opportunity 1 — Rapidly Expanding Asia-Pacific Deodorant Market

The Asia-Pacific deodorant market generated approximately USD 7.15 billion in revenue in 2025, with a projected CAGR of 6.76%, supported by economic growth and rising purchasing power. :fn-ref{#5}

##### Opportunity 2 — E-Commerce Lowering Market-Entry Barriers

The rapid growth of e-commerce and social commerce in the Asia-Pacific allows emerging personal-care brands to reach consumers without relying entirely on traditional retail distribution networks. This lowers the start-up cost and allows us to reach our target consumers through a more diverse set of channels. In addition, e-commerce is particularly well-suited for deodorant products in China, as some consumers may feel embarrassed about purchasing body odor-related products in physical stores, according to our interview with the CEO of KleanK, one of China's leading deodorant brands.

##### Challenge 1 — Price Sensitivity and Intense Competition

Economic pressure is increasing consumer focus on value for money, while the Asia-Pacific personal-care market is already highly competitive, making pricing difficult for new entrants. :fn-ref{#14}

#### Social

##### Opportunity 1 — Widespread Concern about Body Odor

Body odor remains a widespread concern among consumers, creating sustained demand for effective deodorant products. Our survey, which collected 581 responses across 19 countries, further reflects this concern, with over 30% of respondents expressing worries about their body odor. This presents an opportunity for Expelliodor to address existing consumer needs through an innovative deodorant solution.

##### Opportunity 2 — Demand for Cleaner and More Sustainable Personal-Care Products

Asia-Pacific consumers, particularly younger demographics, are increasingly interested in clean, sustainable, and transparent personal-care products, creating opportunities for fragrance-free and targeted deodorant formulations. Some of our interviewed consumers, such as Mr. Wood from our school, expressed concerns about how traditional deodorants work (e.g., aluminum salt-based products block the sweat glands). This might indicate a potential sub-market for deodorant products with minimal physiological intervention.

##### Challenge 1 — Body Odor Remains Culturally Sensitive

Body odor is a socially sensitive and sometimes stigmatized topic across many Asia-Pacific cultures. Its cultural and social meaning diverges even within China. This makes consumer communication and product positioning more difficult; more resources need to be put into understanding the cultural context of different regions before product promotion.

#### Technological

##### Opportunity 1 — Advances in Biotechnology Enabling Targeted Odor Control

Advances in protein engineering, synthetic biology, and bioactive cosmetic ingredients create opportunities to develop deodorants that act on specific odor-producing pathways rather than simply masking odor. The axillary odor-producing pathway we are targeting has only been elucidated relatively recently, with PepV identified in 2024 as a key enzyme in this pathway.  :fn-ref{#15} :fn-ref{#16} This creates a potential first-mover advantage for our project, as, to our knowledge, there are currently no deodorant products specifically developed to target this pathway.

##### Opportunity 2 — Digital Technologies Accelerating Product Development and Commercialization

AI-assisted R&D, digital regulation, and e-commerce infrastructure can shorten development cycles and improve consumer access across the Asia-Pacific. For instance, the engineered proteins we used were designed using an AI-assisted workflow, which reduced research costs during the project's initial phase.

##### Challenge 1 — Stability and Large-Scale Manufacturing of Bioactive Ingredients

Protein-based active ingredients may face challenges in stability, shelf life, and large-scale manufacturing, potentially increasing formulation complexity and production costs compared with conventional deodorants.

### Marketing

#### Marketing Values

Our marketing values center on **choice**, **respect**, and **transparency**.

**Choice**. We believe everyone should be free to decide whether and how to manage body odor. Reflecting this belief, we changed our slogan from “Expell Your Odor” to “Expell Your Way,” shifting the focus from eliminating odor to empowering personal choice.

**Respect**. We reject shame-based marketing that portrays natural body odor as a personal flaw or exploits body-related insecurities to drive market demand.

**Transparency**. As a synthetic biology-based product, Expelliodor requires clear, accessible, and evidence-based communication. We aim to explain our product’s mechanism, disclose relevant safety information, and distinguish validated results from claims that require further testing.

#### Marketing Strategies

##### Digital Advertising

We plan to use platforms such as RedNote, TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube to reach consumers interested in personal care and scientific innovation. Content will feature everyday usage scenarios and product demonstrations, supported by collaborations with skincare influencers to reach relevant audiences.

##### Science Communication

Our promotional content will explain the mechanism in accessible language and address questions about safety, skin compatibility, and how the product differs from conventional deodorants. This is particularly important because the mechanism itself is one of Expelliodor’s main points of differentiation.

##### Product Demonstration and Sampling

Deodorant is ultimately judged through use, so direct experience may be more persuasive than advertising alone.

We therefore plan to conduct product trials and sampling in locations connected to likely use scenarios, including hotels, gyms, and office buildings. Free samples allow consumers to test the product before purchase while also giving us direct feedback on usability and consumer response.

##### Point-of-Purchase Marketing

Our discussion with a representative from Deonatulle drew our attention to the importance of retail visibility.

Expelliodor would therefore aim for eye-level or “golden zone” shelf placement and high-traffic displays where possible. At the point of purchase, packaging should communicate the product function and main differences quickly rather than trying to reproduce the full scientific explanation used online.

##### Roadshows

Roadshows bring several parts of the strategy together. They allow us to demonstrate the product, distribute samples, explain the science, and answer questions directly.

They also give us space to communicate the idea behind **“Expell Your Way”**: body odor is a normal biological characteristic, while choosing whether and how to manage it remains a personal decision.

## Long-term Impacts

### Positive

#### Environmental Friendliness

Our protein-based active ingredients offer a route to biodegradable odor control, as proteins can be broken down into peptides and amino acids.

Microbial production of our fragrance esters provides an alternative to direct plant extraction and enables more controlled production without relying on the seasonal availability of particular aromatic plants.

#### Health and Standards of Living

Expelliodor targets the odor-producing pathway rather than relying on broad bacterial killing. By controlling precursor availability, bacterial uptake and precursor modification, our approach is designed to manage odor while limiting interference with non-target skin microorganisms.

Our formulation contains no aluminum salts or formaldehyde-releasing agents. It therefore avoids exposure to these ingredients and offers an alternative for consumers concerned about their associated irritation or contact-allergy risks.

Our non-surgical approach broadens odor-management options for users who wish to avoid surgery’s cost, recovery period and potential scarring, as well as those who experience odor recurrence after surgery.

We treat odor management as a personal choice and respect people who choose not to use deodorant. Supportive messages in product instructions and non-stigmatizing advertising promote informed choice and challenge prejudice surrounding axillary odor.

#### Inclusive Design

Our packaging addresses difficulties experienced by users with visual impairments or limited grip strength and dexterity, making product information and handling more accessible.

Our adolescent-focused product line prioritizes affordability, convenience and skin compatibility. Different application formats and optional fragrances accommodate varied preferences and routines rather than imposing a single model of personal care.

#### Economic and Social Benefits

Fermentation provides a scalable manufacturing route and opportunities for regional production where suitable facilities exist. This diversifies ingredient sourcing and creates flexibility in future supply-chain development.

Expelliodor creates opportunities for collaboration between protein engineers, fermentation manufacturers, cosmetic formulators and established consumer brands. These partnerships can connect biological innovation with manufacturing expertise, consumer testing and distribution.

An affordable biological alternative expands consumer choice and addresses unmet needs among users dissatisfied with existing deodorants, particularly adolescents and consumers concerned about underarm skin condition.

#### Impact on Related Industries and Markets

Our combination of protein binders and an engineered enzyme expands the design space for deodorants beyond conventional sweat suppression and broad antimicrobial activity. It provides a development framework for targeted biological personal-care ingredients.

Our documented AI-assisted and rational-design workflows provide reusable approaches for generating and evaluating proteins with desired binding or catalytic functions, supporting further innovation in synbio.

By integrating biological performance, accessibility and responsible communication, Expelliodor demonstrates how personal-care innovation can address both functional needs and the social experiences of its users.

### Negative

#### Safety

##### Risk

Some users may develop irritation or allergic reactions to the engineered proteins, fragrance esters or other formulation ingredients, including after repeated use.

##### Management

We will evaluate irritation and sensitization risks before launch and provide clear ingredient lists and usage precautions. We plan to supply individually packaged trial samples through offline retailers and sell sample sizes online so consumers can assess initial skin tolerance before purchasing a full-size product. We also plan to offer a fragrance-free version without our terpene ester module.

#### Stability

##### Risk

Proteins may unfold, aggregate or lose activity during storage and transportation. Temperature fluctuations, agitation and contact with packaging surfaces may contribute to instability; the effects of repeated roll-on use will also require evaluation.

##### Management

We will evaluate trehalose as a potential stabilizer, drawing on its use in other protein formulations, and determine a suitable concentration for our proteins. Before release, we will test activity retention in the final packaging during storage and regular use.

#### Consumer Perception

##### Risk

Users may perceive the product as less effective than antiperspirants since it does not stop sweating. Unfamiliarity with protein-based ingredients may also affect acceptance.

##### Management

Packaging will clearly explain that Expelliodor controls odor without being designed to suppress sweating. Product testing will support our effectiveness claims, while consumer trials will help us improve communication and address concerns.

#### Production Scale

##### Risk

Protein expression and fragrance-ester production optimized in the laboratory may perform differently in larger bioreactors. Changes in oxygen transfer, mixing and process control could lower yields or increase purification costs, making it harder to compete with conventional deodorants on price.

##### Management

We plan to scale production progressively through 5 L and 50 L bioreactors, adjusting fermentation and purification conditions at each stage. Yield, purity, batch consistency and production cost will guide process optimization and pricing decisions.

#### Regulatory Requirements

##### Risk

We intend to develop Expelliodor as a cosmetic deodorant. However, our engineered proteins may require assessment under China’s cosmetic new-ingredient framework. Ingredient safety documentation, efficacy substantiation and applicable registration or notification requirements could increase development costs and delay market entry.

##### Management

We will seek early regulatory guidance, confirm ingredient status and the appropriate product category, and prepare the required safety and efficacy documentation. Product claims will remain focused on cosmetic odor control, with market entry planned around completion of the applicable regulatory requirements.
