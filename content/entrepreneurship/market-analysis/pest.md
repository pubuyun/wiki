## Political

### Opportunity 1 — APEC 2026 Shenzhen

APEC 2026, hosted by China with Shenzhen as a major meeting hub, emphasizes “Openness, Innovation and Cooperation”, with priorities including regional connectivity, digital economy, and support for SMEs (Small and Medium-sized Enterprises). As Expelliodor's headquarters are based in Shenzhen, the company may benefit from the increased regional connectivity and business opportunities associated with APEC. China’s Deputy Foreign Minister Ma Zhaoxu expressed confidence in the further development of the *Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific* (FTAAP), as well as in strengthening cooperation and capacity-building among SMEs. :fn-ref{#10} APEC might lead to further reductions in tariffs and promote a multilateral trading system. This may create a more favorable environment for innovative consumer biotechnology products to expand across Asia-Pacific markets. :fn-ref{#11}

### Opportunity 2 — Reforms in *NMPA*'s Regulations for Cosmetics

China’s *NMPA* has introduced reforms to facilitate the approval of cosmetics with new efficacy claims and to support technological innovation in new cosmetic ingredients, thereby reducing regulatory barriers for innovative formulations. :fn-ref{#12} This could allow our newly synthesized proteins, designed to precisely target 3M3SH for deodorant effects, to benefit from a more supportive regulatory environment, potentially lowering early-stage commercialization barriers and strengthening investor confidence in the product’s development prospects.

### Challenge 1 — Fragmented Regulations across Regional Markets

Cosmetic regulatory frameworks vary considerably across countries, with differences in product classification, ingredient restrictions, safety assessment, labeling, and registration requirements. As a result, compliance achieved in one market cannot necessarily be transferred directly to another, increasing the time, cost, and complexity of international expansion. :fn-ref{#13}

## Economic

### Opportunity 1 — Rapidly Expanding Asia-Pacific Deodorant Market

Asia-Pacific generated approximately USD 6.91 billion in deodorant revenue in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 7.32 billion in 2026, supported by economic growth and rising purchasing power. :fn-ref{#14}

### Opportunity 2 — E-commerce Lowering Market-entry Barriers

The rapid growth of e-commerce and social commerce in Asia-Pacific allows emerging personal-care brands to reach consumers without relying entirely on traditional retail distribution networks. This lowers the start-up cost and allows us to reach our target consumers through a more diverse set of channels. In addition, e-commerce is particularly well-suited for deodorant products in China, as some consumers may feel embarrassed about purchasing body odor-related products in physical stores, according to our interview with the CEO of KleanK, one of China's leading deodorant brands.

### Challenge 1 — Price Sensitivity and Intense Competition

Economic pressure is increasing consumer focus on value for money, while the Asia-Pacific personal-care market is already highly competitive, making pricing difficult for new entrants. :fn-ref{#15}

## Social

### Opportunity 1 — Growing Awareness of Personal Hygiene and Wellness

Rising awareness of personal hygiene, health, and wellness is increasing consumer demand for deodorants and other daily personal-care products across Asia-Pacific. Our survey, which contains 581 samples, also reflects this trend, with more than 30% of participants expressing concerns about their body odor.

### Opportunity 2 — Demand for Cleaner and More Sustainable Personal-care Products

Asia-Pacific consumers, particularly younger demographics, are increasingly interested in clean, sustainable, and transparent personal-care products, creating opportunities for fragrance-free and targeted deodorant formulations. Some of our surveyed consumers, such as Mr. Wood from our school, expressed concerns about how traditional deodorants work (e.g., aluminum salt-based products block the sweat glands). This might indicate a potential sub-market for deodorant products with minimal physiological intervention.

### Challenge 1 — Body Odor Remains Culturally Sensitive

Body odor is a socially sensitive and sometimes stigmatized topic across many Asia-Pacific cultures. Its cultural and social meaning diverges even within China. This makes consumer communication and product positioning more difficult; more resources need to be put into understanding the cultural context of different regions before product promotion.

## Technology

### Opportunity 1 — Advances in Biotechnology Enabling Targeted Odor Control

Advances in protein engineering, synthetic biology, and bioactive cosmetic ingredients create opportunities to develop deodorants that act on specific odor-producing pathways rather than simply masking odor. The axillary odor-producing pathway we are targeting has only been elucidated relatively recently, with PepV identified in 2024 as a key enzyme in this pathway. :fn-ref{#16} :fn-ref{#17} This creates a potential first-mover advantage for our project, as, to our knowledge, there are currently no deodorant products specifically developed to target this pathway.

### Opportunity 2 — Digital Technologies Accelerating Product Development and Commercialization

AI-assisted R\&D, digital regulation, and e-commerce infrastructure can shorten development cycles and improve consumer access across Asia-Pacific. For instance, the engineered proteins we used were designed using an AI-assisted workflow, which reduced research costs during the project's initial phase.

### Challenge 1 — Stability and Large-scale Manufacturing of Bioactive Ingredients

Protein-based active ingredients may face challenges in stability, shelf life, and large-scale manufacturing, potentially increasing formulation complexity and production costs compared with conventional deodorants.
